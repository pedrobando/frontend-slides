# Compass splits the market as rivals unite

Compass, Inc. spent 2025 and 2026 becoming the largest residential brokerage group in the United States, and it is now fighting most of the rest of the industry over who controls listing data. Its all-stock purchase of Anywhere Real Estate closed on **January 9, 2026**, months ahead of guidance. The deal put Coldwell Banker, Century 21, Sotheby's International Realty, Corcoran, ERA and Better Homes and Gardens under a parent that handled about **29% of verified US transaction sides and 39% of sales volume in 2025** once franchise brands are counted. The operating business is strong, with record Q2 2026 revenue of **$4.31B** and **$92M** in GAAP net income. The stock still sits near $9 under a New York attorney general probe, a securities class action over how the deal cleared federal review, and a lawsuit war with MLSs that followed Robert Reffkin's September 30 ultimatum. The listing fight has changed shape too. Most new Compass listings now launch as public Coming Soon homes on Compass.com and Redfin with days on market and price cuts hidden, so the argument has moved from hidden homes to hidden history. On **October 26, 2026**, Keller Williams, AGNT, HomeServices of America and Real REMAX Group answered with **Open Real Estate**, a free broker-owned exchange that would pool members' listings within one business day and split data revenue equally per listing. It exists only on paper, has no MLS partner, and does not include Compass inventory, so it helps by widening access among everyone else rather than by opening Compass's listings. For reThought Real Estate the strongest position costs almost nothing. Sign the free letter of support, give every seller in both states a written "public by default, private only by your signed choice" policy before Wisconsin's law takes effect on January 1, 2027, sell bilingual service in the mid-priced bands where full exposure matters most, and recruit unsettled agents on leadership rather than technology.

## Compass bought its way to 29% of verified transaction sides

Compass grew by acquisition long before Anywhere. It closed the **$444M** purchase of @properties and Christie's International Real Estate on January 13, 2025, then bought Washington Fine Properties in D.C., PorchLight in Denver and Cottingham Chalk in Charlotte during 2025 ([Compass Q1 2025 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1563190/000156319025000095/comp-20250331.htm); [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/washington-dc/2025/02/20/compass-acquires-washington-d-c-luxury-brokerage/); [Compass press release](https://www.compass.com/newsroom/press-releases/uvaoTtVvIzxrDRw4VoIxH/)). On September 22, 2025 it announced the big one. Each Anywhere share converted into 1.436 Compass shares, worth $13.01 and an 84% premium, for an equity value of about **$1.6B** and a combined enterprise value near **$10B** once Anywhere's debt is included ([AP via News4Jax](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2025/09/22/rivals-compass-and-anywhere-real-estate-to-combine-in-deal-valued-at-10-billion-including-debt/); [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/09/22/compass-to-acquire-anywhere-creating-worlds-largest-brokerage)). Some headlines used other figures, including $4.2B, so any slide should quote the deal with its definition. Compass first guided to a close in the second half of 2026 ([Compass Q3 2025 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1563190/000156319025000196/comp-20250930.htm)). It closed on January 9 instead, creating Compass International Holdings with Reffkin as chairman and CEO ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-closes-1-6b-anywhere-merger-forms-industry-giant/)).

The speed is where the trouble starts. The Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period reportedly expired on January 2 without a DOJ Second Request ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102n4w2/new-york-attorney-general-targets-compass-in-antitrust-investigation-after-1-6b/)). The Wall Street Journal, as summarized by Bisnow, reported that Compass hired Trump-allied lawyer Mike Davis, who helped it reach Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche over the head of the antitrust division ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/residential/after-feds-pass-10b-compass-and-anywhere-merger-faces-state-antitrust-probe-134869)). Senators Warren and Wyden had urged close review in December 2025, and a February 20, 2026 letter from 18 Democrats pressed Attorney General Bondi on the handling ([Sen. Wyden](https://www.wyden.senate.gov/news/press-releases/wyden-warren-press-trump-administration-over-compass-anywhere-real-estate-merger-sound-alarm-on-impact-to-housing-costs); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/real-estate-merger-scrutiny/)). New York Attorney General Letitia James's office confirmed an antitrust investigation of Compass's New York dominance on June 3, and the stock closed down about **12%** that day ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/06/03/ny-ag-probing-compass-antitrust-concerns/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/compass-inc-shares-fall-on-report-of-ny-ag-antitrust-probe-1)). On October 7, former Anywhere shareholders filed *Magloire v. Compass* in federal court in Manhattan, alleging the merger registration statement hid the Davis campaign. Compass says the claims lack merit, and the lead-plaintiff deadline is December 8 ([WRE News](https://wrenews.com/compass-anywhere-merger-shareholder-lawsuit-disclosures/); [Kessler Topaz](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/25/3387750/0/en/compass-inc-comp-class-action-lawsuit-investors-face-december-8-2026-deadline-kessler-topaz-meltzer-check-llp.html)). Lead independent director Charles Phillips resigned the same week, citing outside commitments and no disagreement, and former Google Gemini head Sissie Hsiao joined the board on October 14 ([Compass 8-K](https://investors.compass.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001563190-26-000157/comp-20261007.htm)). A DOJ spokesperson has said nothing prevents future enforcement, so divestiture risk in markets like Manhattan has not gone away ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102n4w2/new-york-attorney-general-targets-compass-in-antitrust-investigation-after-1-6b/)).

The size claims need careful wording. RealTrends data compiled by HousingWire puts Compass alone at **5.9% of sides and 11% of volume** in 2025, and the full ecosystem including franchised Anywhere brands at **29.13% of sides and 39.44% of volume**, or $940.3B ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-anywhere-real-remax-exp-market-share/)). Those shares measure the RealTrends-verified universe of about 4.16 million sides, which is not every home sale in the country ([RealTrends 500](https://www.realtrends.com/ranking/500-by-volume/?page=0)). Counting only company-owned brokerages, the Consumer Policy Center puts Compass and Anywhere at about **18% of US volume**, with far higher shares in target metros ([Consumer Policy Center](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-expansion-report-April-2026.pdf)). T3 Sixty says the new Compass is larger than the brokerages ranked third through seventh combined ([T3 Sixty via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/compass-leads-mega-1000-amid-accelerating-consolidation-302736510.html)). The top ten firms' share of volume nearly doubled from 14.3% in 2017 to 27.2% in 2024, mostly through acquisitions ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/12/22/trends-2026-the-brokerage-feeding-frenzy-and-market-concentration)).

Integration has been a cost-cutting exercise, and on paper it is working. Compass had actioned **$330M** in year-one net cost synergies by August, five months early, against a three-year target of $500M ([Compass Q2 2026 release](https://investors.compass.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/177/compass-inc-reports-record-second-quarter-2026-results)). A New Jersey WARN notice cut 110 jobs at Anywhere's Madison headquarters, and Anywhere's CEO and CTO left as the merger agreement provided ([NJBiz](https://njbiz.com/compass-110-jobs-madison-anywhere-merger/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-anywhere-ceo-exit/)). One acquired brand pruned low producers, so owned-brokerage agents fell from 84,187 to 83,184 in Q2 on 3,819 separations and 2,816 additions ([Compass Q2 2026 release](https://investors.compass.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/177/compass-inc-reports-record-second-quarter-2026-results)). Compass began moving its AI Home Platform to company-owned Coldwell Banker Realty, Corcoran, Sotheby's and @properties agents on July 2, with franchisees promised access in 2027 ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/07/02/compass-rolls-out-ai-powered-home-platform-across-company-owned-brokerage-brands/)). Brands are already being reshuffled. Coldwell Banker Warburg folded into Compass in July and reopened as Christie's International Real Estate in New York in September ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/09/08/warburg-transitions-to-cire-raleigh-team-joins-compass)), and MYNY, the largest Coldwell Banker franchise in New York, went independent in April ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/04/23/why-top-coldwell-banker-franchise-went-independent/)). Clients also met a nationwide flat transaction fee in February 2026. A Palm Beach County couple sued over a **$475** Compass fee in June, then dropped the case without prejudice in August, which leaves them free to refile ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/06/26/compass-transaction-fees-under-fire-in-new-class-action-suit); [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/08/11/compass-florida-transaction-fee-lawsuit-update-dismissed/)).

The financials show strength. Revenue rose to **$6.96B** in 2025, with a $58.5M net loss and $293.4M of adjusted EBITDA ([Compass Q4 2025 release](https://investors.compass.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/88/compass-inc-reports-record-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results)). Q2 2026, the first combined quarter not propped up by a one-time tax benefit, brought $4.31B in revenue, $92M in net income, $363M of adjusted EBITDA and $180M of free cash flow, against $3.14B of long-term debt ([Compass Q2 2026 release](https://investors.compass.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/177/compass-inc-reports-record-second-quarter-2026-results)). The stock still slid from about $12.50 in August to roughly **$9** by mid-October ([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9128198/compass-comp-files-antitrust-lawsuit-against-california-regional-mls); [Levi & Korsinsky](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/26/3388398/3080/en/comp-shareholder-alert-compass-inc-securities-class-action-lawsuit-investors-should-contact-levi-korsinsky.html)). No source ties the slide to a single event. Q3 results arrive November 5 ([Morningstar via PR Newswire](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20261016ny73937/compass-inc-to-announce-third-quarter-results-on-november-5)). Anyone who tells agents Compass is collapsing is wrong. Its risks are legal and political, not operational.

## The listing war moved from hidden homes to hidden history

Compass sells sellers a three-phase plan. A home starts as a Private Exclusive that only Compass-family agents and their clients can see. It then becomes a Compass Coming Soon listing shown publicly without days on market or price history, and only after that does it reach the MLS ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/03/18/compass-drops-lawsuit-after-zillow-embraces-pre-marketing)). In February 2025, 35% of Compass's 22,138 listings were in one of the first two phases ([Real Estate News](https://realestatenews.com/2025/02/19/compass-flexes-private-inventory-market-share-as-revenue-soars)). After the merger, Compass tells buyers its private exclusives reach the roughly 340,000 agents in its network ([Compass.com](https://www.compass.com/private-exclusives/buyers/)). Reffkin also told agents that Anywhere brands would face no mandate to use them ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/01/09/what-reffkin-told-agents-after-compass-anywhere-deal-closed)).

Through 2026 the weight shifted to the public Coming Soon phase. A three-year alliance with Rocket, announced February 26, put Compass Coming Soon listings on Redfin with premium placement and no days on market, price history or value estimate. It also offered buyers with Compass agents a one-point rate cut in year one or up to $6,000 in Rocket Mortgage credits ([Rocket](https://ir.rocketcompanies.com/news-and-events/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/Compass-and-Rocket-Form-Historic-Alliance-to-Dramatically-Increase-Home-Listing-Inventory-on-Redfin/default.aspx)). In July 2026, **57% of new Compass listings launched as Coming Soon**, and Reffkin expected about 80% by the end of Q3 ([MikeDP](https://library.mikedp.com/content/the-battle-for-unique-inventory-is-moving-on-from-private-listings)). On September 22 Compass added See It First, a Compass.com section for pre-market listings from all nine brands that HousingWire reports any buyer's agent can view and ask about ([HousingWire](https://housingwire.com/articles/compass-com-see-it-first)). Analyst Mike DelPrete reads this as two tools with two jobs. Private exclusives help Compass recruit and keep agents, while Coming Soon pulls consumer traffic and leads ([MikeDP](https://library.mikedp.com/content/the-battle-for-unique-inventory-is-moving-on-from-private-listings)).

The private phase still matters because it keeps both sides of the deal in-house. A Compass internal slide produced in the Zillow litigation said off-market sales double-end **72% more often** than on-market sales. In a Consumer Policy Center sample of 1,000 Washington D.C. sales, Compass represented both sides in **41%** of its deals, against 11% to 18% for rivals ([Consumer Policy Center](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-Market-Report-4.15.26.pdf)). When DelPrete's researchers posed as outside buyer agents asking about Compass private exclusives, **90%** of their requests were ignored or refused ([Consumer Policy Center](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-Market-Report-4.15.26.pdf)). Compass also pays a listing agent 10% of the commission when they hand a buyer inquiry to another Compass agent who closes the sale ([Consumer Policy Center](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-Market-Report-4.15.26.pdf)).

Compass lost both of its emergency court fights and still got most of what it wanted through deals. Zillow's Listing Access Standards, announced in April 2025, banned listings that were publicly marketed but not placed on the MLS within a business day. Compass sued in June 2025, and Judge Jeannette Vargas denied its injunction on February 6, 2026, finding it unlikely to win on the merits ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/judge-denies-compass-injunction/)). Zillow launched Zillow Preview for sanctioned pre-marketing on March 17, and Compass dropped the suit the next day ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/03/18/compass-drops-lawsuit-after-zillow-embraces-pre-marketing)). Preview grew to more than 400 brokerage partners, including Keller Williams, RE/MAX and HomeServices, and Realtor.com agreed to carry the listings ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/05/05/zillow-realtor-com-partner-on-pre-market-listings/); [Zillow](https://zillow.mediaroom.com/2026-05-05-Zillow-and-Realtor-com-R-set-a-new-standard-for-pre-market-transparency,-extending-Preview-listings-to-buyers-across-both-platforms)). Compass settled its 16-month fight with Northwest MLS on August 31 in exchange for a new 21-day First Look status that keeps days on market and early price changes off public view ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/08/31/compass-nwmls-settle-16-month-legal-battle)). In Chicago, Zillow sued the MRED MLS and Compass in May. Judge John Tharp denied Zillow's injunction on September 15 and sent the claims against MRED to arbitration ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/09/15/court-denies-zillow-in-mred-case/)).

NAR never repealed Clear Cooperation. On March 25, 2025 it kept the one-business-day rule and added a delayed-marketing option backed by a seller disclosure, which every MLS had to adopt by September 30, 2025 ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2025/03/25/breaking-nar-not-alter-clear-cooperation-adds-new-exempt-designation/); [NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/summary-of-2025-mls-changes)). Its July 9, 2026 guidance said the office-exclusive option belongs to the seller, required brokers to explain every option and get informed written instructions, and named Wisconsin, Washington and Connecticut as states with their own pre-marketing rules ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-office-exclusive-mls-guidance/)).

Then came open war. Compass sent demand letters in early September. At the Council of MLS conference on September 30, Reffkin told MLSs to stop fining agents who publicly market office exclusives by October 6 or face antitrust suits seeking treble damages, and he said Compass would not settle ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reffkin-sue-mls-exclusives/)). California Regional MLS sued first on October 5, asking a Manhattan federal court to declare its rules lawful ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/10/06/crmls-compass-rules-listings-antitrust/)). Compass sued CRMLS in Los Angeles on October 16 over fines of up to **$2,500**. It offered to pay those fines for Compass, Coldwell Banker Realty and Sotheby's agents who assign Compass their right to sue ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/10/16/compass-sues-crmls-over-listing-policies/)). OneKey MLS in New York refused on October 20. On October 21 six MLSs from Arizona, Colorado, North Carolina and the San Francisco Bay Area sued Compass for a declaration that their cooperation rules are lawful ([Yahoo Finance via OneKey](https://finance.yahoo.com/real-estate/articles/onekey-mls-rejects-compass-demand-100000093.html); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/mls-sue-compass-antitrust/)). Compass counters that MLSs serving more than 350,000 agents in 12 states already allow what it wants, a claim no one has verified ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/mls-sue-compass-antitrust/)). The Council of MLS is raising a legal fund of more than $1M ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/10/23/cmls-forming-1m-plus-legal-action-fund-after-compass-threats)). No court has ruled in any of these cases.

States moved faster than federal regulators, and they converged on one model. Washington's SB 6091, in effect since June 2026, requires private marketing to run alongside public marketing unless the owner's health or safety requires otherwise ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/washington-private-listing-networks-law/)). Connecticut's SB 340 took effect October 1 with a standardized seller opt-out form and $5,000 fines ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/connecticut-private-listing-law/)). **Wisconsin enacted a law in December 2025, effective January 1, 2027, that makes public online marketing the default within one business day of a listing agreement unless the seller signs an opt-out** ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/washington-private-listing-networks-law/)). New York's legislature passed a similar bill in June, and whether the governor has signed it is unconfirmed ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/20/ny-private-listing-bill-awaits-governor-signature-focused-on-informed-choice/)).

The consumer evidence is weaker than either side admits. Zillow's studies find off-MLS homes sold for 1.3% to 1.5% less, with a 4.0% gap in majority-Hispanic neighborhoods ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2025/03/24/zillow-report-finds-private-listing-networks-result-in-lower-sale-prices/); [Zillow](https://zillow.mediaroom.com/2026-05-14-Selling-with-one-agent-on-both-sides-cost-home-sellers-1-49-billion-over-three-years)). Compass's July 2026 study claims a 4.6% premium for homes that started as Private Exclusive or Coming Soon, but it excluded expired and withdrawn listings and released no data ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/07/24/compass-zillow-private-exclusives-data-research-listings/)). A University of Georgia study of more than 700,000 Dallas-Fort Worth listings found a 1.7% premium for private listings, though the paper has since come down from SSRN ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/13/how-dueling-real-estate-data-studies-shaping-private-listings-fight/)). DelPrete has called the evidence far from conclusive either way ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/06/16/the-private-listings-debate-leaves-more-questions-than-answers)). The studies also measure different homes. Compass counts pre-marketed homes that later sold on the MLS, while Zillow counts homes that never reached it. Both findings can hold at once. A short pre-market run followed by full exposure may help a seller, while a sale that never leaves one firm's network may hurt. Compass's best argument deserves a fair hearing too. Reffkin says days on market and price-cut history destroy value, and HousingWire's own data showed **42.2%** of active single-family listings carrying a cut ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reffkin-private-exclusives-mls/)). Sellers going through divorce, death or a health crisis also have real privacy reasons, which MRED's CEO groups as the Five Ds ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/05/12/zillow-sues-compass-mred-over-collusion-to-hide-listings)).

## Open Real Estate pools everyone's listings except Compass's

Open Real Estate is a proposed national listing-data exchange. It is not a brokerage, a portal or an MLS. Its founders are AGNT, parent of eXp Realty and NextHome; HomeServices of America, home of the Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices brokerages; Keller Williams; and Real REMAX Group ([Business Wire via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/real-estate/articles/leading-real-estate-firms-partner-134500268.html)). Real REMAX Group is the holding company created by The Real Brokerage's combination with RE/MAX, a deal signed in April 2026 and described as a platform of about 180,000 agents ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/real-brokerage-sets-board-lineup-for-planned-re-max-merger-and-nasdaq-listed-real-remax-group)). Brown Harris Stevens, Lamacchia Realty, Samson Properties, Windermere and Iron Valley signed on as early participants ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/10/26/4-major-brokerage-firms-unveil-open-real-estate-listings-platform)). The FAQ says it is not a national MLS and will work with existing MLSs ([Open Real Estate FAQ](https://openrealestate.com/faq)). A broker enters a listing once using standard fields, other members see it through shared infrastructure, and the exchange syndicates it to MLSs, portals, appraisers and data services ([openrealestate.com](https://openrealestate.com)). The founders' open letter promises it is "not a walled garden" and argues that no single brokerage, portal or trade group should control housing information ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/open-real-estate-listing-access/)).

The rules so far are short. Members submit every public listing, and the FAQ requires entry within one business day of signing the listing agreement ([Open Real Estate FAQ](https://openrealestate.com/faq)). The main site separately says listings that start elsewhere must arrive within one business day of going public, so the exact trigger is still unsettled ([openrealestate.com](https://openrealestate.com)). Brokers pick one of two tiers at entry. A market listing goes public and flows out through MLS feeds. A participant-view listing is visible only to agents at member brokerages, and nobody, including the listing agent, may market it publicly ([Open Real Estate FAQ](https://openrealestate.com/faq)). Joining is free for any licensed residential brokerage. Today joining means signing a non-binding letter of support that puts the firm's name and logo on the site, while individual agents can only sign up for updates ([openrealestate.com](https://openrealestate.com)). Listings stay the property of the listing broker. AGNT CEO Leo Pareja says data revenue will be shared equally per listing, so a 100-listing firm earns the same per listing as a 10,000-listing firm ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/open-real-estate-initiative/)).

It is still a plan. A technology vendor is due to be chosen in Q1 2027, no launch date exists, and sources disagree on timing ([Open Real Estate FAQ](https://openrealestate.com/faq); [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/10/26/4-major-brokerage-firms-unveil-open-real-estate-listings-platform)). The founders admit it will take massive capital, and no MLS has publicly agreed to accept its feed ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/open-real-estate-initiative/); [Chicago Agent](https://chicagoagentmagazine.com/2026/10/26/open-real-estate/)). The Real Deal's Housing Notes column called it the first real competitor to private listings while stressing that it is not yet a working platform ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/10/26/housing-notes-private-listings-meet-their-first-real-competitor/)). Compass was invited to be a founding member and has not joined. A Compass spokesperson nonetheless welcomed a national listing service that hands power back to the homeowner and their agent ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/10/26/4-major-brokerage-firms-unveil-open-real-estate-listings-platform)). The MindCast AI newsletter reads that praise as positioning, since Open Real Estate's entry rule is stricter than the CRMLS rule Compass is suing to kill ([MindCast AI](https://www.mindcast-ai.com/p/compass-open-real-estate-endorsement-without-entry)).

The coalition carries real weight. By my sum of RealTrends figures, AGNT's eXp and NextHome held about 8.3% of 2025 sides and the combined Real and RE/MAX about 12.3%. Those two founders alone cover roughly a fifth of verified sides before counting Keller Williams, or HomeServices, the No. 4 brokerage by 2025 volume at $135.9B ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-anywhere-real-remax-exp-market-share/); [T3 Sixty via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/compass-leads-mega-1000-amid-accelerating-consolidation-302736510.html)). A boutique can point to that network when a Compass agent tells a seller that 340,000 agents will see the home privately.

| Problem for a non-Compass brokerage | What Open Real Estate would do | What it would not do |
|---|---|---|
| Outside agents cannot reach Compass private exclusives | Nothing directly | Compass, Coldwell Banker and Sotheby's are not members |
| Compass pitches sellers a 340,000-agent private network | A participant-view tier visible to agents at every member firm | Nothing is live before 2027 |
| Listings split between Compass.com and Redfin versus Zillow and Realtor.com Preview | One entry syndicated to MLSs and portals | No portal agreements announced, and the exchange alone decides where listings go |
| Small firms hand listing data to MLSs and portals for free | Equal per-listing revenue share | No payout terms published |
| Boutiques lack national allies | Free public membership beside KW, eXp, RE/MAX and HomeServices | The letter is non-binding and governance is unwritten |
| Pre-market listings hide days on market and price cuts | A data-integrity principle and a pledge of informed seller decisions | No stated rule on showing that history |

So the honest answer to how it could help has two parts. It would shrink Compass's network advantage by giving every other firm a shared pool of comparable reach. It would offer a quiet-launch option seen by agents at many competing firms instead of one company, plus a promised share of data revenue on listings boutiques now hand over for free ([openrealestate.com](https://openrealestate.com); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/open-real-estate-initiative/)). It would not open Compass's own inventory, fix portal fragmentation on its own, or stop Compass's Coming Soon listings from hiding price history. Membership also costs some control, because the exchange decides where listings syndicate ([Open Real Estate FAQ](https://openrealestate.com/faq)). One open question matters for reThought in particular. A participant-view listing is marketing to a limited group, and Wisconsin's law will require a seller's signed opt-out for anything short of public marketing. My inference is that Wisconsin agents would need that form before using the tier, though no source has addressed the point.

## South Florida is a Compass stronghold while Wisconsin stays local

Florida and Wisconsin present different fights, and reThought should not run one script in both.

In South Florida the Compass system now owns several of the names buyers see on yard signs. The Compass-owned Sotheby's International Realty franchisor took over ONE Sotheby's International Realty's franchise business around September 9, 2026. That turned a Miami firm with **1,375 agents and $6.85B in 2025 sales** into a company-owned unit ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/09/09/sothebys-2-largest-franchises-are-now-company-owned); [South Florida Agent Magazine](https://southfloridaagentmagazine.com/2026/09/09/sothebys-international-one-sothebys-ttr-sothebys/)). The Real Deal's October ranking, which I could see only as a search snippet, puts Compass first in Miami-Dade, with Coldwell Banker third and ONE Sotheby's fourth ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/magazine/october-2026/these-are-miamis-top-real-estate-agents-and-brokerages/)). A pre-close Capitol Forum analysis named Fort Lauderdale among markets where the combined firm would dominate ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-market-share-analysis/)). Company-owned Coldwell Banker Realty, now part of Compass, runs offices in Tampa Westshore, South Tampa, New Tampa, Lakewood Ranch, Palm Harbor, southwest Orlando, Lake Mary and Winter Park ([Coldwell Banker Realty Tampa](https://www.coldwellbankerhomes.com/fl/tampa/offices/); [Coldwell Banker Realty Orange County](https://www.coldwellbankerhomes.com/fl/orange-county/offices/)). Many Coldwell Banker and Century 21 offices elsewhere in Florida are independent franchisees, so reThought should not imply that every one answers to Compass.

Florida's MLS rules vary by market. Stellar MLS, which covers Tampa Bay and Orlando, enforces Clear Cooperation strictly. Agents get five business days to enter a listing or file an exclusion, and one business day after any public marketing. Its delayed-distribution option is capped at **five calendar days**, social media marketing during an exclusion counts as a violation, and fines reach **$15,000, charged to the agent rather than the broker** ([Stellar MLS Clear Cooperation](https://www.stellarmls.com/clearcooperation); [Stellar MLS Delayed Distribution](https://www.stellarmls.com/DelayedDistribution)). Compass's September demand letter named Miami REALTORS MLS among eight MLSs that, in Compass's view, already allow public marketing of office exclusives, and no Florida MLS is among those suing Compass ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/09/30/compass-crmls-legal-fight-mls/); [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/10/22/compass-sued-by-six-more-multiple-listing-services/)). WAV Group counts Stellar, Miami and Emerald Coast among MLSs whose cooperative models have pushed office exclusives to some of the lowest levels in the country ([WAV Group](https://www.wavgroup.com/2026/07/23/clear-cooperation-is-encouraging-office-exclusives-and-reducing-mls-cooperation/)). This research found no Florida statute like Wisconsin's.

Florida's bilingual opportunity is large and sits in the right price bands. The state has the third-largest Hispanic population in the country at 6.7 million, and Latino buyers are among the fastest-growing segments in Miami, Orlando and Tampa, mostly in entry-level and mid-priced homes ([Florida Realtors](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/03/hispanic-buyers-power-florida-home-sales)). Hispanic households made up 92.6% of US household growth in 2025 ([NAHREP](https://nahrep.org/press-releases/2026/02/10/us-census-bureau-hispanics-post-net-gain-441000-homeowners-in-2025/)). Those are the price points where off-MLS marketing offers the least and broad exposure matters most.

Wisconsin is different ground. Compass entered Milwaukee in August 2021 with 16 agents, then added Keefe Real Estate in Lake Geneva and a Lake Country team ([Compass](https://investors.compass.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/161/compass-expands-to-wisconsin); [BizTimes Milwaukee](https://biztimes.com/compass-adds-real-estate-agents-in-lake-country-lake-geneva)). Metro MLS listings in 2026 show Compass offices in the North Shore, Tosa and Lake Geneva ([Shorewest listing page](https://www.shorewest.com/WI/Milwaukee/Milwaukee/606-W-Wisconsin-Ave-1801-Milwaukee-WI-53203/1963992-METRO)). The merger adds a company-owned Coldwell Banker Realty office in Milwaukee, and the @properties deal brought a Christie's affiliate in Madison whose current status I could not verify ([Coldwell Banker Realty Milwaukee](https://www.coldwellbankerhomes.com/wi/milwaukee/office/milwaukee/oid_238/); [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2023/03/29/properties-christies-expand-to-madison-wisconsin-with-affiliate/)). Shorewest, family-owned since 1946, and First Weber remain the big local names, and neither appeared in any Compass deal coverage ([Clever](https://listwithclever.com/real-estate-blog/shorewest-realtors-review/)). This research found no market-share data for Milwaukee, Madison, Racine or Kenosha.

Wisconsin's cooperative infrastructure is unusually unified. Metro MLS enforces Clear Cooperation and offers an option that shows a listing only to MLS members ([Metro MLS](https://metromls.com/the-truth-about-clear-cooperation-policy/)). Seven Wisconsin MLSs share listings through WIREX, which covers almost 90% of MLS-listed homes in the state ([SCWMLS](https://scwmls.com/wirex/)). The Wisconsin REALTORS Association framed the 2025 NAR changes as opportunities rather than obstacles and published Addendum LM as the state's seller-options form ([WRA, June 2025](https://www.wra.org/WREM/Jun25/MLS/); [WRA, August 2025](https://www.wra.org/WREM/Aug25/MLOS/)). No Wisconsin MLS appears in Compass's demand list or in the lawsuits ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/09/30/compass-crmls-legal-fight-mls/)). The January 1, 2027 law is the change that matters most here.

## reThought should sell exposure, language and leadership, not size

### One written promise for sellers, in English and Spanish

Clients choose people, not logos. In NAR's 2025 buyer and seller profile, **66% of sellers** used an agent they had been referred to or had used before, and 81% interviewed only one agent ([Virginia REALTORS summary of NAR](https://virginiarealtors.org/2025/12/08/key-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers/)). Agents remain buyers' most trusted information source, and 91% of sellers used one ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/nar-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-sellers-reveals-market-extremes)). Compass's scale matters less in that contest than it looks. What reThought needs is a promise its clients can repeat to friends.

That promise should be a written exposure policy used in both states. Every listing goes to the MLS and out to the portals within one business day unless the seller chooses otherwise in writing after seeing what they give up. The paperwork already exists in Stellar's Multiple Listing Options form in Florida and Addendum LM in Wisconsin ([Stellar MLS](https://www.stellarmls.com/DelayedDistribution); [WRA](https://www.wra.org/WREM/Aug25/MLOS/)). The policy matches NAR's July guidance, the Washington, Connecticut and Wisconsin laws, and Open Real Estate's own entry rule. Adopting it in Florida gives reThought one standard across both offices. Adopting it in Wisconsin before January 1 turns a legal requirement into a selling point.

The policy should not concede "seller choice," because that is Compass's best argument. reThought agents can offer pre-marketing that stays inside the rules. Stellar allows a five-day delayed distribution with the seller's signature, Metro MLS has a members-only option, and Zillow Preview now has more than 400 brokerage partners, so reThought should ask whether a small firm can join ([Stellar MLS](https://www.stellarmls.com/DelayedDistribution); [Metro MLS](https://metromls.com/the-truth-about-clear-cooperation-policy/); [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/05/05/zillow-realtor-com-partner-on-pre-market-listings/)). When a seller worries about looking stale, the honest answer is pricing. More than 42% of active listings carry a price cut, and the way to avoid one is to price right at launch, not to hide the history ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reffkin-private-exclusives-mls/)).

The fiduciary argument should rest on Compass's own evidence and on neutral sources. Compass's internal slide on double-ending, the Consumer Policy Center's 41% D.C. figure and the 90% refusal rate in DelPrete's test all point the same way. A private listing can serve the brokerage more than the seller ([Consumer Policy Center](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-Market-Report-4.15.26.pdf)). WAV Group estimates that about 98% of homes still trade through the MLS ([WAV Group](https://www.wavgroup.com/2026/07/23/clear-cooperation-is-encouraging-office-exclusives-and-reducing-mls-cooperation/)). Avoid leaning on Zillow's numbers alone, since Zillow is Compass's courtroom opponent.

The bilingual angle is sharper than it first looks. In a 2025 Zillow survey, 74% of Hispanic sellers and 73% of Black sellers said their agent recommended a private listing network, against 24% of white sellers ([Banker & Tradesman summary](https://bankerandtradesman.com/?p=224739)). Zillow's price study found the largest off-MLS gap, 4.0%, in majority-Hispanic neighborhoods ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2025/03/24/zillow-report-finds-private-listing-networks-result-in-lower-sale-prices/)). Zillow is an interested party, so present these as reasons for caution rather than proof. Even so, a Spanish-language walk-through of the seller-options form and a plain promise of *exposición total, en su idioma* gives a Hispanic seller in Orlando or Tampa something a luxury-focused private network does not.

Buyers need a different message. No single website shows everything anymore. Compass Coming Soon homes appear on Compass.com and Redfin, rival pre-market homes appear on Zillow and Realtor.com Preview, and in Chicago MRED is free to cut its listings from Zillow ([Rocket](https://ir.rocketcompanies.com/news-and-events/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/Compass-and-Rocket-Form-Historic-Alliance-to-Dramatically-Increase-Home-Listing-Inventory-on-Redfin/default.aspx); [Zillow](https://zillow.mediaroom.com/2026-05-05-Zillow-and-Realtor-com-R-set-a-new-standard-for-pre-market-transparency,-extending-Preview-listings-to-buyers-across-both-platforms); [RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/09/15/court-denies-zillow-in-mred-case/)). reThought buyer agents should monitor Compass's See It First page, which HousingWire says outside agents can query, and tell buyers that their agent searches every channel ([HousingWire](https://housingwire.com/articles/compass-com-see-it-first)). If reThought does not charge a flat client transaction fee, it should say so, since Compass's nationwide fee drew a Florida class action ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/06/26/compass-transaction-fees-under-fire-in-new-class-action-suit)).

### Agents move for leaders, not software

reThought does not need to match Compass's Home Platform. In a 2025 1000Watt survey, **40%** of agents who switched firms cited better leadership or culture and 23% cited splits, while marketing, technology, training and help with industry change together came to 17% ([Recruiting Insight](https://recruitinginsight.net/2025/05/27/the-real-reason-agents-change-brokerages/)). A smaller, company-sponsored Real Brokerage survey in June 2026 ranked economics first at 25% and lead generation second at 19% ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/real-estate/articles/real-monthly-agent-survey-competitive-130000014.html)). Lead with broker access and culture, then fair and transparent economics.

Recruit patiently. After the deal was announced, 53% of Anywhere agents told a Zillow survey they would leave or consider leaving under Compass, though Zillow did not disclose the sample size ([Zillow via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/half-of-anywhere-real-estate-agents-say-they-would-consider-leaving-if-compass-acquisition-moves-forward-302607784.html)). The measured exit was about 1.2% net in Q2 at owned brokerages, much of it Compass pruning low producers ([Compass Q2 2026 release](https://investors.compass.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/177/compass-inc-reports-record-second-quarter-2026-results)). Consultant Steve Murray advises rivals to watch for teams breaking apart during integration, and Douglas Elliman's CEO is waiting for agents to call rather than poaching ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-anywhere-agent-strategies/)). For reThought the targets are Coldwell Banker Realty agents in Tampa, Orlando and Milwaukee, ONE Sotheby's agents adjusting to company ownership, and Coldwell Banker and Century 21 franchise agents once the Home Platform and other changes reach them in 2027. The window runs from now through mid-2027.

Answer the "bigger network" objection with affiliations. Joining Open Real Estate is free. LeadingRE gave MYNY relocation, referral and market-data reach after it left Coldwell Banker ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/04/23/why-top-coldwell-banker-franchise-went-independent/)), and analyst Rob Hahn suggests the Top Agent Network for agent-to-agent off-market intelligence ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-anywhere-agent-strategies/)).

Train agents on the rules that hit them personally. Stellar fines the agent, up to $15,000, and bans social media teasers during an exclusion ([Stellar MLS](https://www.stellarmls.com/DelayedDistribution)). Compass's offer to pay fines covers only agents at its own brands, and only those who assign Compass their right to sue ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/10/16/compass-sues-crmls-over-listing-policies/)). Wisconsin agents need training on the new law and Addendum LM before January 1.

Keep the talk track accurate. Compass posted a record Q2 and is profitable, so do not tell agents it is failing. The lobbying and antitrust claims are allegations in pending cases. When citing the 29% and 39% shares, say "counting franchise brands." The reassuring numbers favor reThought anyway. **53% of REALTORS** work at independent firms, and the country still has about 100,000 brokerages ([NAR 2026 Member Profile](https://www.lirealtor.com/docs/default-source/default-document-library/research-and-industry-reports/2026-nar-member-profile.pdf); [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/12/22/trends-2026-the-brokerage-feeding-frenzy-and-market-concentration)).

The next checkpoints are Compass's Q3 results on November 5, the December 8 lead-plaintiff deadline, any ruling in the CRMLS or six-MLS cases, Wisconsin's law on January 1, Open Real Estate's vendor pick in Q1 2027, and the Home Platform's franchise rollout during 2027.

## Conclusion

The industry is not splitting into a private camp and an open camp, whatever the press releases say. Both camps now offer a private tier. Compass's runs inside one company with nine brands, and Open Real Estate's participant view runs across many competing firms. The real question for a seller is how many competing agents see the home, and whether the seller chose that in writing after seeing the trade-offs. That framing is more accurate than "private is bad," it survives Compass's best arguments, and it matches the law reThought's Wisconsin office will have to follow in ten weeks. Regulators, NAR and Open Real Estate have all landed on the same standard of public by default and private only by informed, signed choice. A boutique that adopts it now in both states looks principled instead of reactive.

The larger risk for reThought is not losing listing presentations to Compass. Most sellers hire someone they were referred to or have used before, and they rarely interview a second agent. The risk is invisibility if inventory splinters into networks a small firm does not belong to. Free membership in Open Real Estate, disciplined MLS entry and a referral affiliation cost little and hedge against that outcome. Most of the open questions, from the MLS suits to Open Real Estate's technology and the Home Platform's reach into franchises, start resolving in the first half of 2027, so reThought should build its recruiting push and Spanish-language seller materials for that window rather than wait for the courts.
