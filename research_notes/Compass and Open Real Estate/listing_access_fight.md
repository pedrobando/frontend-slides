# The fight over listing access and Compass "private exclusives" (Jan 2025 to Oct 26, 2026)

Scope note: US only. Compass corporate financials and M&A are covered only where they change how listings are marketed. After the Anywhere deal closed in January 2026, the parent company files and sues as "Compass International Holdings," and some sources use that name. Sources marked "(company claim)," "(party allegation)," or "(opinion)" are not independent findings.

## 1. What Compass's "private exclusives" and three-phased marketing strategy is, and how it changed through 2026 (including after the Anywhere deal)

### Takeaway
In plain terms, Compass tells sellers to market the home in three steps. First it is a "Private Exclusive," which only Compass agents and their clients can see. Then it becomes a "Coming Soon" listing on Compass's own site (and, since March 2026, on Redfin), shown without days on market or price-cut history. Only after that does it go on the MLS, the shared database that every brokerage and the big portals use. Compass says this gives sellers a choice and keeps a home from looking "stale." Critics say the early phases shrink the pool of buyers, steer deals so that Compass represents both sides, and hide price history that buyers would want. Over 2026 Compass moved its weight from the private phase to the Coming Soon phase (57% of new listings in July 2026) and built outside distribution through Redfin and its own "See It First" page. After the Anywhere merger, Compass said it would not require the brands it bought to use private exclusives.

### Inferences
- The "private exclusive" label now matters less than the "Coming Soon" phase. Since the Redfin deal and See It First, much of Compass's pre-MLS inventory is publicly visible but stripped of days on market and price-cut history. The fight has moved from "hidden listings" to "who controls the listing data and what buyers see about a home's history."
- Private exclusives still matter for double-ending. Compass's own slide and the CPC data point to the private phase as the step that keeps both sides of a deal in-house.
- Anywhere tripled the pool of agents a private exclusive can reach inside the company. The 340,000 figure is also what Compass now cites when it says the listings are not really "private." For an outside agent, the network got bigger but they are still outside it.

### Gaps
- I found no 2026 figure for the share of all Compass listings (as opposed to new listings) that start as Private Exclusives, and no figure for Anywhere-brand adoption.
- I could not get the numbers from the Inman Intel survey (Feb 9, 2026) because Inman pages returned 403 errors.
- Whether Private Exclusives actually reached Redfin after the Coming Soon phase started there is not confirmed in what I retrieved.

## 2. NAR's Clear Cooperation Policy, the 2025 "Multiple Listing Options for Sellers" change, and status as of October 2026

### Takeaway
NAR did not repeal Clear Cooperation. On March 25, 2025 it kept the rule (a listing must go into the MLS within one business day of any public marketing) and added a "delayed marketing exempt listing" option plus clearer office-exclusive rules. MLSs had to put these in place by Sept 30, 2025. In 2026 NAR issued more guidance (May 21 and July 9) but left enforcement to local MLSs. As of October 2026 the rule is still on NAR's books. Compass openly refuses to follow it, and some large MLSs (CRMLS, Bright, NWMLS) have widened their Coming Soon or pre-marketing statuses. Commentators called the policy "dead" in May 2026, but that is opinion. On paper it is not repealed.

### Cited findings
- Mar 25, 2025: NAR kept Clear Cooperation and added a new "delayed marketing exempt listing" designation under a policy called "Multiple Listing Options for Sellers." Sources: [RISMedia, Mar 25, 2025](https://www.rismedia.com/2025/03/25/breaking-nar-not-alter-clear-cooperation-adds-new-exempt-designation/); [NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/nar-introduces-new-flexibility-for-sellers-while-retaining-clear-cooperation-policy)
- Local MLSs had until Sept 30, 2025 to implement it. The policy left in place the Clear Cooperation requirement to file a listing within one business day of public marketing. An office exclusive is filed with the MLS but not shared with other participants. Sources: [NAR summary of 2025 MLS changes](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/summary-of-2025-mls-changes); [NAR model rule changes PDF, Apr 28, 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-04/nar-model-mls-rule-changes-for-exempt-listing-2025-04-28.pdf)
- May 21, 2026: NAR guidance defined "one-to-one" communication as directly telling one other agent or broker about a listing, verbally or in writing. Source: [HousingWire, Jul 13, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-office-exclusive-mls-guidance/)
- July 9, 2026: NAR guidance on broker duties. It says the office-exclusive option "belongs entirely to the seller." Brokers must explain all options and get the seller's informed instructions plus disclosures, including the seller's acknowledgment of the MLS benefits being waived or delayed. A listing must still be submitted within one business day if it is publicly marketed or discussed with outside agents in anything other than one-to-one contact. Even with one-to-one contact, the receiving broker must agree not to market or show the property. MLSs set their own active/non-active definitions and enforce Clear Cooperation themselves. They must make all active listings available through a VOW feed, which NAR said followed prior discussions with the US Department of Justice. NAR flagged Wisconsin, Washington, and Connecticut as states with laws or rules on pre-marketing. Source: [HousingWire, Jul 13, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-office-exclusive-mls-guidance/); also [Inman, Jul 10, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/07/10/nar-office-exclusive-pre-marketing-broker-duties/)
- As of 2026, NAR's handbook still carries Clear Cooperation and NAR restated it in July 2026 guidance. Compass has said it "has not and will not adhere to CCP or any national NAR MLS rule." This comes from a secondary training site, so the quote needs checking. Source: [Jamil Academy](https://www.jamilacademy.com/blog/clear-cooperation-policy)
- An Inman piece of May 15, 2026 was headlined "The Clear Cooperation Policy Is Dead. What Comes Next?" (opinion; I could not read the text). Source: [Inman, May 15, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/05/15/the-clear-cooperation-policy-is-dead-what-comes-next/)
- CRMLS and Bright MLS, the two largest MLSs, widened their Coming Soon rules in June and July 2026. Sellers can now keep listings off portals while brokers still share them across the MLS. Source: [MikeDP, Aug 10, 2026](https://library.mikedp.com/content/the-battle-for-unique-inventory-is-moving-on-from-private-listings)
- At the Sept 30, 2026 CMLS conference, Reffkin cited NAR's 1971 14-Point Antitrust Policy. He quoted it as saying an MLS "shall not make any rule regulating the advertising or promotion of any listings." He also said MLSs that enforce rules after NAR's policy change "last November" face greater liability. Source: [HousingWire, Sept 30, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reffkin-sue-mls-exclusives/)

### Inferences
- Clear Cooperation still exists, but in practice it has become a patchwork of local MLS rules. MLSs that widened pre-marketing statuses (NWMLS, CRMLS, Bright) have taken in part of what Compass wants while still requiring MLS submission. Compass's October 2026 lawsuits aim at the remaining piece: fines for agents who publicly market a listing outside the MLS.
- NAR's July 2026 guidance put a lot of weight on seller disclosure and informed consent. That matches the opt-out-with-disclosure model in the new state laws (section 5).

### Gaps
- I could not confirm what "NAR's policy change last November" (November 2025) refers to in Reffkin's Sept 30, 2026 remarks. Searches turned up no November 2025 NAR board action on office exclusives. It may refer to a revised office-exclusive definition, but that is unverified.
- I found no NAR statement of its own on the October 2026 lawsuits between Compass and the MLSs.
- A source mentions an "American Real Estate Association" petition to repeal Clear Cooperation, but it is low-quality, so I left it out.

## 3. Zillow's Listing Access Standards, Compass v. Zillow, Compass v. NWMLS, and other MLS and portal actions

### Takeaway
Compass lost its main court fights but mostly got what it wanted through deals. A federal judge in New York refused to block Zillow's listing ban (Feb 6, 2026). Then Zillow launched its own pre-marketing product, "Zillow Preview," and Compass dropped the suit (Mar 18, 2026). Compass settled with Northwest MLS on Aug 31, 2026, getting a new 21-day "First Look" status. In Chicago, Zillow sued Compass and the local MLS (MRED) and lost its injunction bid on Sept 15, 2026. That ruling lets MRED cut off Zillow's feed and sends Zillow's MLS claims to arbitration. Since September 2026 Compass has been threatening and suing MLSs that fine agents. MLSs are suing first to get courts to declare their rules lawful.

### Cited findings

#### Zillow Listing Access Standards and Compass v. Zillow (S.D.N.Y.)
- April 2025: Zillow announced Listing Access Standards. Listings that are publicly marketed but not widely available through the MLS (the policy uses one business day) are barred from Zillow. Enforcement began in late June 2025. Sources: [Real Estate News, Mar 18, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/03/18/compass-drops-lawsuit-after-zillow-embraces-pre-marketing); [HousingWire, Feb 6, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/judge-denies-compass-injunction/)
- June 2025: Compass sued Zillow, alleging monopoly power and antitrust violations. It later alleged a Zillow-Redfin conspiracy after Redfin moved to adopt a similar ban, which never took effect. Source: [Real Estate News, Mar 18, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/03/18/compass-drops-lawsuit-after-zillow-embraces-pre-marketing)
- Feb 6, 2026: Judge Jeannette Vargas (S.D.N.Y.) denied Compass's preliminary injunction in a 50-page decision, finding "Compass has not shown a likelihood of success on the merits." On cross-examination, Compass's head of investor relations conceded there was no concrete evidence linking the three-phase plan to revenue changes, and that Compass revenue kept rising after the policy was announced. Reffkin said, "Today's decision is not a loss." Sources: [HousingWire, Feb 6, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/judge-denies-compass-injunction/); [Wilson Sonsini (Zillow's counsel) summary](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/compass-voluntarily-dismisses-antitrust-suit-against-zillow-after-firm-defeats-preliminary-injunction.html)
- Mar 17, 2026: Zillow introduced "Zillow Preview," which publicly pre-markets listings with brokerage branding and priority placement in search and saved-home alerts. Preview periods are limited by local MLS rules, which in some markets means 24 hours. Zillow lowered its referral fee on Preview buyer deals from 35% to 25%, with the other 10% going to the listing agent. Sources: [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-preview-public-premarketing/); [HousingWire, Apr 10, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/premarketing-listings-compass-zillow-exp/)
- Zillow said its standards "remain fully in effect" alongside Preview. It also clarified that it would not ban listings publicly marketed first on Compass's sites or on Redfin. Sources: [Inman, Mar 17, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/03/17/zillow-preview-will-exist-in-tandem-with-listing-access-standards/); [Real Estate News, Mar 18, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/03/18/compass-drops-lawsuit-after-zillow-embraces-pre-marketing)
- Mar 18, 2026: Compass voluntarily dismissed the suit without prejudice, so it could refile. Reffkin wrote: "Because of this reversal, we are dismissing our lawsuit against Zillow." Zillow called the suit "meritless" and said private listing networks "undermine fair access to real estate information." Sources: [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/03/18/compass-drops-lawsuit-after-zillow-embraces-pre-marketing); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/18/business/compass-zillow-lawsuit-listing-ban); [Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/news/compass-abandons-lawsuit-affirming-zillow-pro-consumer-listing-standards/)
- Zillow Preview adoption: 58 brokerages and franchisors had signed agreements by Apr 10, 2026. Zillow later said it had more than 400 brokerage partners. Keller Williams, RE/MAX, HomeServices of America, and United Real Estate are among them. Sources: [HousingWire, Apr 10, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/premarketing-listings-compass-zillow-exp/); [The Real Deal, May 5, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/05/05/zillow-realtor-com-partner-on-pre-market-listings/); [CPC report, p. 13](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-Market-Report-4.15.26.pdf)

#### Realtor.com, Redfin/Rocket, Homes.com

Sources: [Zillow press release, May 5, 2026](https://zillow.mediaroom.com/2026-05-05-Zillow-and-Realtor-com-R-set-a-new-standard-for-pre-market-transparency,-extending-Preview-listings-to-buyers-across-both-platforms); [Real Estate News, Sept 17, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/09/17/zillow-realtor-com-make-commitment-to-openness-with-preview)

Source: [search summary of RISMedia, Feb 26, 2026](https://ace.rismedia.com/2026/02/26/compass-redfin-rocket-partnership-q4-2025-earnings/)
- Homes.com and Realtor.com agreed to carry pre-sale listings for eXp. eXp syndication began April 15, 2026. Sources: [CPC report, p. 13](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-Market-Report-4.15.26.pdf); [HousingWire, Apr 10, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/premarketing-listings-compass-zillow-exp/)

#### Compass v. NWMLS (W.D. Wash., Seattle)
- April 2025: Compass sued Northwest MLS, alleging anticompetitive practices, obstruction of seller choice, and retaliation. NWMLS temporarily suspended Compass's IDX feed in April 2025. NWMLS moved to dismiss in June 2025, and a judge denied the motion in March 2026. In April 2026, NWMLS counterclaimed under the Washington Consumer Protection Act, calling the three-phase strategy a "deceptive scheme" and citing the new state law. Source: [Real Estate News, Aug 31, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/08/31/compass-nwmls-settle-16-month-legal-battle); see also [HousingWire on denial of dismissal](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-nwmls-dismissal-denied/)
- One search summary says NWMLS cut off Compass's data feed starting at the end of March 2026 until Compass agreed to follow its standards. Real Estate News describes only the April 2025 suspension. The two may be separate events, but this is unconfirmed. Source: [The Real Deal, Aug 31, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/08/31/compass-northwest-listing-service-settle-antitrust-lawsuit/)
- Aug 31, 2026 settlement terms:
  - New "First Look" status effective Sept 4, 2026, treated like Coming Soon. It allows up to 21 days of pre-launch marketing with showings, open houses, and offers.
  - Days on market and pre-launch price changes are not shown publicly. MLS members can still see them.
  - Sellers choose whether First Look listings go to IDX sites.
  - By Oct 15, portals must prominently display the listing broker, and NWMLS must stop watermarking photos.
  - By Nov 15, NWMLS must add the data fields brokers need.
  - Compass says the deal bars NWMLS from suing Compass "under the guise of 'enforcing state law'" (Compass characterization).
  - Reffkin said the goal "has been fully realized." Redfin's Joe Rath praised NWMLS.
  Sources: [Real Estate News, Aug 31, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/08/31/compass-nwmls-settle-16-month-legal-battle); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-nwmls-coming-soon-settlement/); [RISMedia, Aug 31, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/31/breaking-compass-nwmls-reach-settlement-bitter-private-listing-lawsuit/)

#### Zillow v. MRED and Compass (N.D. Ill., Chicago)
- May 12, 2026: Zillow sued MRED (Chicago's MLS) and Compass under the Sherman Act, alleging a group boycott and monopoly abuse. The following are Zillow's allegations, not findings. MRED and Compass coordinated to threaten cutting off Zillow's Chicago feed unless Zillow displayed Compass listings it had banned. MLS Grid, which runs MRED's feed, made that threat. Compass holds 23% of MRED's board seats and 35% of Chicago sales. Compass agreed to subsidize MRED membership for the first 100,000 Compass agents who join. Sources: [Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/news/zillow-sues-mred-compass/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/zillow-mred-compass-lawsuit/); [Chicago Agent, May 12, 2026](https://chicagoagentmagazine.com/2026/05/12/zillow-files-antitrust-lawsuit-against-mred-compass/)
- MRED cut Zillow's feed on May 20, 2026. On May 22, Judge John Tharp ordered MRED to restore Zillow's IDX and VOW feeds under conditions. A two-day hearing began July 1. Sources: [Chicago Agent, May 22, 2026](https://chicagoagentmagazine.com/2026/05/22/judge-orders-mred-to-restore-zillows-access-to-listing-data-amid-antitrust-lawsuit-against-mred-compass/); [The Real Deal, Jul 1, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/chicago/2026/07/01/zillows-fight-with-mred-compass-over-listings-heads-to-court/)
- MRED CEO Rebecca Jensen defended MRED's Private Listing Network as a quiet option for sellers facing "the Five Ds: Divorce, Death, Disability, Displacement, Downsizing." Source: [search summary citing HousingWire/Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/05/12/zillow-sues-compass-mred-over-collusion-to-hide-listings)
- Sept 15, 2026: Tharp denied Zillow's preliminary injunction in a 54-page opinion. He found Zillow had not made a strong showing on any element (agreement, unreasonable restraint, antitrust injury). He compelled arbitration of the claims against MRED and stayed the claims against Compass. The ruling dissolved the restraining order, so MRED can cut Zillow off. Zillow said, "Today's ruling is not the final word." Compass said the decision "affirms that choice belongs to the homeowner." ### Inferences
- The three court results so far run in opposite directions. Compass could not stop Zillow's ban, and Zillow could not stop MRED's cutoff. Judges have been unwilling to give either side emergency relief on antitrust theories. - Both sides of the October 2026 MLS suits have filed for declaratory judgment, mostly in S.D.N.Y. The court that decides whether MLS cooperation rules are lawful may matter more than any single case.
- For agents outside Compass, the practical change is that most big portals and many MLSs now have a sanctioned pre-market status (Zillow/Realtor.com Preview, NWMLS First Look, CRMLS and Bright Coming Soon). Every brokerage can now pre-market. The open question is whether any of these statuses hide days on market and price history from the public.

## 4. Evidence on consumer impact: prices, double-ending, access, and fair housing

### Takeaway
The price evidence is contested and comes almost entirely from interested parties. Zillow-backed studies find off-MLS or private sales go for about 1.3% to 1.5% less, with bigger gaps in Black and Hispanic neighborhoods (3.1% to 4.0%). Compass's studies find pre-marketed homes sell for 2.9% (Feb 2025) to 4.6% (July 2026) more. One academic study Compass cites found a 1.7% premium. None of these has been independently replicated, and they measure different things. Compass counts homes that went through all three phases and sold on the MLS, while Zillow counts homes that never hit the MLS. The double-ending evidence is clearer. Compass's own slide says off-market sales double-end 72% more often. A consumer-group sample found Compass double-ending 41% of its DC sales against 11% to 18% for rivals.

### Cited findings

#### Price studies
- Zillow, Mar 24, 2025, 2023 to 2024 data: off-MLS sellers got about 1.5% less, a median of $4,975. The gap was 3.1% ($5,576) in majority-Black neighborhoods, 4.0% ($13,728) in majority-Hispanic neighborhoods, and 1.2% in majority-white neighborhoods. The dollar figure for white neighborhoods is reported as $3,694 by Zillow/Inman and $3,964 by RISMedia. Zillow said sellers lost about $1 billion in total. Sources: [RISMedia, Mar 24, 2025](https://www.rismedia.com/2025/03/24/zillow-report-finds-private-listing-networks-result-in-lower-sale-prices/); [Inman, Mar 24, 2025](https://www.inman.com/2025/03/24/homesellers-have-lost-1b-through-private-deals-since-2023-zillow/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/home-sellers-off-mls-listings-lost-1-billion-sale-proceeds-zillow/)
- Same Zillow 2025 survey: 74% of Hispanic sellers and 73% of Black sellers said their agent recommended a private listing network, against 24% of white sellers. Source: [search summary of Banker & Tradesman / Zillow](https://bankerandtradesman.com/?p=224739)
- Zillow, May 14, 2026, more than 15 million transactions, 2023 to 2025: off-MLS sellers sold for 1.3% less, about $4,230 per home and $1.36 billion combined. Dual-agency sellers (one agent on both sides) lost about $2,165 per home and $1.49 billion combined, with California the largest at about $533 million. Sources: [Zillow press release, May 14, 2026](https://zillow.mediaroom.com/2026-05-14-Selling-with-one-agent-on-both-sides-cost-home-sellers-1-49-billion-over-three-years); [Real Estate News, May 14, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/05/14/sellers-lose-over-2k-on-typical-dual-agency-transaction-study); [RISMedia, Aug 13, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/13/how-dueling-real-estate-data-studies-shaping-private-listings-fight/)
- Zillow expert Lawrence Wu (court declaration, early July 2026, Zillow v. MRED/Compass): Compass private-listing-network sales were linked to prices 4.0% lower in Chicagoland and 4.8% lower nationally. He used a narrower definition of private sale than Compass. Sources: [The Close](https://theclose.com/news-compass-zillow-private-listing-data/); [RISMedia, Aug 13, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/13/how-dueling-real-estate-data-studies-shaping-private-listings-fight/)
- Compass, Jul 24, 2026: 70,809 closed transactions from April 2025 to March 2026. Homes that started as Private Exclusive or Coming Soon sold for 4.6% more than comparable homes listed straight on the MLS, up from 2.9% in its February 2025 study. They went under contract 34% faster after MLS activation and were 29% less likely to have a price cut (company claims). The study excluded expired and withdrawn listings, claimed controls for 50+ variables without listing them, and did not release data for replication. Sources: [Inman, Jul 24, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/07/24/compass-zillow-private-exclusives-data-research-listings/); [The Close](https://theclose.com/news-compass-zillow-private-listing-data/); [RISMedia, Aug 13, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/13/how-dueling-real-estate-data-studies-shaping-private-listings-fight/)
- Compass "Zillow tax" study, July 2026: homes that appeared on Zillow sold for 1.3% less (sale-to-list ratio) than comparable homes that did not (company claim). Critics note that the 806 banned homes came from a shorter window than the roughly 296,000 comparison homes. Sources: [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-zillow-tax/); [Real Estate News, Jul 31, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/07/31/compass-data-suggests-listing-on-zillow-comes-at-a-cost)
- Darren Hayunga (University of Georgia), more than 700,000 Dallas-Fort Worth listings over 20 years: a 1.7% premium for private listings, less than half of Compass's figure. He attributed it to a "negotiation discount" for MLS sellers. The paper is no longer on SSRN ("under review or removed"). Source: [RISMedia, Aug 13, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/13/how-dueling-real-estate-data-studies-shaping-private-listings-fight/)
- Philadelphia Fed working paper WP 25-35 (2025): for sellers 70 and older, private listings led to lower sale prices. Zillow's Mischa Fischer also cites a peer-reviewed Journal of Real Estate Economics paper with similar conclusions. Source: [RISMedia, Aug 13, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/13/how-dueling-real-estate-data-studies-shaping-private-listings-fight/)
- Critiques of the Compass study. Fischer (Zillow chief economist) says Compass's sample is mostly homes that failed to sell privately and then sold on the MLS, so the 4.6% partly reflects Compass targeting higher-value homes. Wayne Bell (Real Estate Advocacy Alliance) says the results cannot be verified because they rest on internal data. Sources: [RISMedia, Aug 13, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/13/how-dueling-real-estate-data-studies-shaping-private-listings-fight/); [The Real Deal / Housing Notes, Jul 27, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/07/27/compass-made-a-report-saying-private-listings-sell-for-more/)

Homes marketed on the MLS sold for 16.84% more than office exclusives, and office exclusives took 31 days to go under contract vs 11. A 2022 update put the on-MLS premium at 14.8% for 2021. These are MLS-funded studies, and critics question causation. Source: [Inman, Jul 30, 2021](https://www.inman.com/2021/07/30/homes-listed-in-the-mls-sell-faster-and-for-nearly-17-more-study); [Inman, Aug 31, 2022](https://www.inman.com/2022/08/31/mls-listed-homes-sell-for-more-a-new-study-claims-but-theres-1-twist/)

#### Compass's case ("seller choice")
- Reffkin on CNBC (Oct 13, 2026): "days on market is the killer of value, price drop history is the killer of value." He said 43% of homes on the market in September had a price cut in their history. HousingWire's own data showed 42.2% of active single-family listings with a cut. He also claimed 6% of homes were pulled last month "because sellers don't want to look stale" (HousingWire did not verify this). He said private listings are "just private from real estate websites that put these negative insights on your listing," and that MLSs "are dictating how we can and cannot compete in the living room." Source: [HousingWire, Oct 14, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reffkin-private-exclusives-mls/)
- Compass's position on the MLS suits is "Only the law and the homeowner should tell agents how to publicly market a home." Source: [RISMedia, Oct 22, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/10/22/six-mlss-sue-compass-seeking-block-lawsuits-affirm-rules/)
- MRED's Jensen and NAR both frame private options as serving sellers' privacy and safety. NAR says the office-exclusive choice "belongs entirely to the seller." Sources: [HousingWire, Jul 13, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/nar-office-exclusive-mls-guidance/); [Real Estate News, May 12, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/05/12/zillow-sues-compass-mred-over-collusion-to-hide-listings)

#### Critics' case (transparency, steering, fair housing)
- Canopy MLS CEO Anne Marie DeCatsye questioned how "fair" pre-marketing is and said removing days on market and other stats would be "confusing" for consumers. Source: [RISMedia, Oct 22, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/10/22/six-mlss-sue-compass-seeking-block-lawsuits-affirm-rules/)
- Brown Harris Stevens CEO Bess Freedman said "hidden listings... create a real challenge" for consumers. Reffkin acknowledged that the NAACP and other civil rights groups had raised concerns. Source: [HousingWire, Oct 14, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reffkin-private-exclusives-mls/)
- The CPC argues that buyers are losing information about time on market and price changes on Compass and other private listings. It also argues that as Compass grows, sellers will feel pushed to list with Compass and buyers will feel they need a Compass agent to see new listings. Source: [CPC report, p. 4 and 14-15](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-Market-Report-4.15.26.pdf)

### Inferences
- The most solid consumer-harm evidence is about double-ending, not price. Compass's own internal slide and the CPC data line up. Whether double-ending hurts sellers depends on whether the agent extracts a lower price or higher combined fee. Zillow's $2,165-per-home dual-agency estimate is the only dollar figure I found, and it comes from an interested party.
- The two sides measure opposite groups. Compass counts homes that were pre-marketed and then sold on the MLS. Zillow counts homes that never reached the MLS. Both numbers could be true together: a short pre-market stage followed by the MLS may help, while a sale that never leaves the private network may hurt. That reading fits Compass's own move from Private Exclusives to Coming Soon.
- For buyers outside Compass, the cost is information. They see fewer listings in the first phase and less history (days on market, price cuts) in the Coming Soon phase. For outside agents, the cost is access, as the DelPrete "ghosting" test suggests. For sellers, the evidence is mixed, but the fair housing data (larger gaps and more agent recommendations in Black and Hispanic areas) is the strongest equity argument against private networks.

### Gaps
- No independent, peer-reviewed study of Compass's own transactions exists in what I found. Every Compass-specific price figure comes from Compass or a Zillow expert.
- I found no 2025 to 2026 Bright MLS or Redfin study specific to Compass, and no formal National Fair Housing Alliance or HUD position on private exclusives.
- I found no national figure for the share of all US listings marketed privately in 2026.

## 5. State legislation and regulator action through October 2026

### Takeaway
States moved faster than federal regulators. Washington (signed March 2026, in effect June 2026) requires private marketing to run alongside public marketing, with a health and safety exception. Connecticut (signed May 27, 2026, effective Oct 1, 2026) and Wisconsin (enacted December 2025, effective Jan 1, 2027) make public marketing the default but let sellers opt out with a signed form. New York passed a similar bill in June 2026, but I could not confirm that the governor signed it. Illinois and Hawaii bills are pending. Federal antitrust regulators have not acted on private listings. Members of Congress and, reportedly, the New York attorney general have asked questions about Compass's size.

### Cited findings
- Washington SB 6091 was introduced in January 2026 and backed by Washington Realtors. It was signed around Mar 16, 2026 (HousingWire's report misspells Gov. Bob Ferguson's name) and took effect in early to mid June 2026. It bars marketing a home to a limited or exclusive group unless the property is "concurrently marketed to the general public" and to all other brokers. The exception is restrictions "reasonably necessary to protect the health or safety" of the owner or occupant. Sources: [HousingWire, Mar 17, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/washington-private-listing-networks-law/); [Real Estate News, Jan 13, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/01/13/washington-realtors-backs-bill-targeting-private-listings); [Real Estate News, Aug 31, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/08/31/compass-nwmls-settle-16-month-legal-battle). Real Estate News says the law's vagueness makes its practical effect uncertain, and NWMLS cited it in its April 2026 counterclaim against Compass.
- Connecticut SB 340 was signed by Gov. Ned Lamont (May 27, 2026 per RISMedia) and takes effect Oct 1, 2026. It requires public marketing of 1 to 4 unit residential listings unless the seller signs a standardized opt-out form, and private-channel marketing must be accompanied by concurrent public marketing. Violations carry a $5,000 fine and possible loss of license. Sources: [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/connecticut-private-listing-law/); [RISMedia, Aug 20, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/20/ny-private-listing-bill-awaits-governor-signature-focused-on-informed-choice/); [News12, Jun 5, 2026](https://bronx.news12.com/house-hunting-new-state-law-could-mean-more-real-estate-listings)
- Wisconsin enacted a law in December 2025, effective Jan 1, 2027. It makes public online marketing the default within one business day of a listing agreement, with an opt-out form. Source: [HousingWire, Mar 17, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/washington-private-listing-networks-law/)
- New York S10274, the "Fair and Transparent Real Estate Listings Act," passed the Senate on Jun 1, 2026 (amended to add a seller opt-out) and passed both houses in early June. It requires timely public marketing, allows private marketing only with the seller's informed written direction after a state disclosure, and carries fines up to $5,000. It would take effect 180 days after signing. As of Aug 20, 2026 it was awaiting delivery to Gov. Hochul. Sources: [RISMedia, Aug 20, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/08/20/ny-private-listing-bill-awaits-governor-signature-focused-on-informed-choice/); [HGAR](https://www.hgar.com/realtors-hail-passage-of-bill-restricting-private-home-listings-in-new-york-state); [The Real Deal, June 2026](https://therealdeal.com/policy-pro/new-york/2026/06/new-york-advances-pocket-listings-crackdown/)
- Illinois HB3452 (Rep. Lilian Jiménez, 2025) would write Clear Cooperation into the Real Estate License Act, with display within one calendar day. It was in committee in 2025, and Illinois Realtors said it was not on their agenda. HousingWire still listed Illinois as pending in March 2026, along with Hawaii. Sources: [Inman, May 8, 2025](https://www.inman.com/2025/05/08/illinois-bill-seeks-to-make-clear-cooperation-into-law/); [Real Estate News, May 7, 2025](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/05/07/a-way-to-end-private-listings-fight-make-clear-cooperation-the-law); [HousingWire, Mar 17, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/washington-private-listing-networks-law/)
- Federal and state regulators:
  - 18 members of Congress asked the DOJ for more information about the Compass-Anywhere deal. Source: [Inman, Feb 20, 2026, cited in CPC fn. 5](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-Market-Report-4.15.26.pdf)
  - As of April 2026, the CPC saw "no indication" that DOJ or the FTC planned antitrust action, and no state attorney general action. Source: [CPC report, p. 14](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-Market-Report-4.15.26.pdf)
  - An August 2026 HousingWire opinion column says a House Judiciary subcommittee asked Compass to explain its MRED partnership, and that the New York attorney general's office is "reportedly" reviewing the Anywhere deal. Both claims are unconfirmed. Source: [HousingWire column, Aug 20, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-results-mls-claims/)
  - A Capitol Forum analysis reportedly found "potentially illegal overlaps" in at least 12 states. The CPC could not obtain or verify it. Source: [Real Estate News, Dec 26, 2025, cited in CPC fn. 18](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-Market-Report-4.15.26.pdf)

### Inferences
- The state laws mostly codify "public by default, private by signed opt-out." That model sits close to NAR's July 2026 disclosure guidance and does not ban Compass's strategy outright. Washington's concurrent-marketing rule is the strictest and may be why the NWMLS settlement looks the way it does: a pre-market status inside the MLS, not outside it.
- The CPC notes that the state bills were pushed partly by Compass competitors who had just signed up to pre-list with Zillow. Support for "transparency" laws is not purely consumer-driven.

### Gaps
- New York S10274 signing status as of Oct 26, 2026 is unconfirmed.
- I found no 2026 Illinois or Hawaii floor votes.
- I found no confirmed DOJ, FTC, or state AG enforcement action on private listings specifically.

## 6. What is the headline in September to October 2026?

### Takeaway
The current headline is Compass's legal campaign against MLSs that fine agents for publicly marketing listings outside the MLS, and the MLSs' pre-emptive countersuits. The sequence runs from demand letters in early September, through the Sept 30 CMLS threat and the Oct 6 deadline, to CRMLS suing on Oct 5, Compass suing CRMLS on Oct 16, and six MLSs suing Compass on Oct 21. Alongside that are Compass's "See It First" push on Compass.com (Sept 22), the NWMLS First Look status going live (Sept 4), the Chicago ruling against Zillow (Sept 15), and Connecticut's law taking effect (Oct 1).

### Cited findings
#### Consolidated timeline (all dates from sources cited above)
- Feb 2025: Compass touts that 35% of its listings are Private Exclusive or Coming Soon and publishes a 2.9% premium study. [Real Estate News](https://realestatenews.com/2025/02/19/compass-flexes-private-inventory-market-share-as-revenue-soars); [The Close](https://theclose.com/news-compass-zillow-private-listing-data/)
- Mar 24, 2025: Zillow study finds off-MLS sales 1.5% lower. [RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2025/03/24/zillow-report-finds-private-listing-networks-result-in-lower-sale-prices/)

### Inferences
- What it means right now:
  - Sellers will face more pitches for "pre-market" options from every major brokerage, not only Compass. In Connecticut and Washington, and Wisconsin from 2027, sellers will see a signed disclosure if they choose private marketing.
  - Buyers who rely on one portal will miss some inventory. Compass Coming Soon listings show on Redfin and Compass.com. Rival pre-market listings show on Zillow and Realtor.com Preview. In Chicago, MRED listings may disappear from Zillow.
  - Non-Compass agents are at a disadvantage on private exclusives, which they may not be able to access (the DelPrete test). They also face a Compass that is 30% to 40% of unit sales in some big metros (CPC). But most of them now have their own pre-marketing channels through Zillow Preview and MLS Coming Soon statuses.
- The pending MLS lawsuits are the next decision point. If a court finds that MLS rules fining agents for off-MLS public marketing are lawful, Clear Cooperation-style rules survive locally. If not, the MLS's role as the single shared database weakens further.
