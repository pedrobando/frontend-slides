## Q1. Market concentration: how much does Compass + Anywhere control, how does it compare with other large players, and what do analysts say it means for independents?

### Takeaway
The combined Compass system had about **29% of US transaction sides and 39% of sales volume in 2025** when franchise brands are counted (RealTrends data). Counting only company-owned brokerages, it had **about 18–19% of volume**. Compass alone is now bigger than the No. 3 through No. 7 brokerages combined. Analysts describe consolidation as "unprecedented." Even so, about **53% of REALTORS still work at independent, non-franchised firms**, and the non-Compass majors (eXp/AGNT, HomeServices, Keller Williams and Real REMAX) are now forming their own coalition (Open Real Estate) against private listings.

### Cited Findings
**Combined share (RealTrends 2025 data, published May 14, 2026)**
- Total 2025 US market: 4,158,305.5 transaction sides and $2.38 trillion volume — [HousingWire, May 14, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-anywhere-real-remax-exp-market-share/)
- **Compass International Holdings ecosystem (Compass + all Anywhere owned and franchised brands): 29.13% of sides, 39.44% of volume ($940.3B)** — [HousingWire, May 14, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-anywhere-real-remax-exp-market-share/). (HousingWire's text says "over 1.2 billion sides," which is a typo for about 1.2 million.)
- Compass standalone, 2025: 245,173 sides (5.90%), $262.23B (11% of volume). Anywhere Advisors (Anywhere's company-owned brokerage): 245,250 sides, $192.76B — [HousingWire, May 14, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-anywhere-real-remax-exp-market-share/)
- eXp World Holdings, 2025: 343,091 sides (8.25% of sides; 8.32% including NextHome), $155.56B (6.53% of volume) — same source
- Real Brokerage, 2025: 131,047 sides (3.15%), $65.22B (2.74%) — same source
- RE/MAX brand, 2025: 378,961 sides, $165.7B. A combined "Real REMAX Group" would hold 12.26% of sides and 9.69% of volume — same source (HousingWire called the combination hypothetical in May 2026. By Oct 26, 2026, "Real REMAX Group" is named as a founding member of Open Real Estate — [Real Estate News, Oct 26, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/10/26/4-major-brokerage-firms-unveil-open-real-estate-listings-platform))
- Owned-brokerage-only view: Compass and Anywhere 2025 sales volume together were "only about 18 percent of total U.S. sales," but far higher in targeted metros — [Consumer Policy Center (Stephen Brobeck), April 2026](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-expansion-report-April-2026.pdf)
- Why the figures differ: the 29%/39% figure counts franchise brands (Century 21, Coldwell Banker, BHGRE and ERA offices, many independently owned). The 18% figure counts owned brokerages only. Compass and Anywhere were expected to argue that franchised businesses should not count in antitrust market-share analysis — [HousingWire, Dec 26, 2025](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-market-share-analysis/)

**T3 Sixty Mega 1000 (2025 US sales volume, released Apr 8, 2026)**
- 1. Compass $262.2B (+13.5%); 2. Anywhere Advisors $196.1B (+5.0%); 3. eXp Realty $155.6B (+1.9%); 4. HomeServices of America $135.9B (−0.5%); 5. Real Brokerage $65.2B (+53.7%); 6. Douglas Elliman $39.8B; 7. Howard Hanna $37.3B; 8. Redfin $32.1B; 9. Side $25.8B; 10. Peerage Realty Partners $24.8B — [T3 Sixty / PR Newswire, Apr 8, 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/compass-leads-mega-1000-amid-accelerating-consolidation-302736510.html)
- Stefan Swanepoel (T3 Sixty): "Compass' position is no longer just about growth." The new No. 1 is larger than the companies ranked third through seventh combined. Jack Miller (T3 Sixty CEO): "scale is no longer built the same way." T3 says the industry is "experiencing consolidation at an unprecedented level" — same source
- Keller Williams does not appear in the brokerage top 10 because it is ranked as a franchise system. I did not find its 2025 share in the sources reviewed (see Gaps).

**Longer-run concentration (T3 Sixty 2026 Swanepoel Trends Report)**
- The top 10 brokerages' share of US sales volume rose from **14.3% (2017) to 27.2% (2024)**, mainly through acquisitions — [Real Estate News, Dec 22, 2025](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/12/22/trends-2026-the-brokerage-feeding-frenzy-and-market-concentration)
- The US still has **roughly 100,000 brokerages**, a number that "may not have meaningfully changed in recent years." Of the 100 largest brokerages in 2018, 27 had been acquired by 2025, mostly by today's top 10 — same source
- Compass bought nine of the 100 largest brokerages by January 2025 — same source
- MLS count fell below 500 in 2025, down 43% in a decade (T3 Sixty Organized Real Estate Indices) [UNCONFIRMED, snippet] — [T3 Sixty 2026 Trends press](https://www.t360.com/press/2026-swanepoel-trends-report-pr)

**Local concentration (where independents feel it most)**
- Consumer Policy Center sample of 1,000 consecutive sales per metro (late 2025 / early 2026). "New Compass" (including Anywhere and @properties) unit-sales share: Boston 32%+, Chicago 35%, San Diego ~30%, Austin 29.7%, Washington DC 39.5%. In each metro this is at least 4x the No. 2 firm (2.5x in Austin). Few other firms reach 3% — [Consumer Policy Center, Apr 2026](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-expansion-report-April-2026.pdf); [Real Estate News, Apr 14, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/04/14/across-the-us-the-new-compass-is-running-away-with-the-lead)
- The largest independents still hold meaningful share locally: Baird & Warner had 8.1% in Chicago ("Chicago's largest independent firm") and Moreland 3.4% in Austin — [Real Estate News, Apr 14, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/04/14/across-the-us-the-new-compass-is-running-away-with-the-lead)
- Compass's stated goal is "30 percent market share in 30 area markets." CPC notes this appears to be measured in sales volume, not units — [Consumer Policy Center, Apr 2026](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-expansion-report-April-2026.pdf)
- Pre-close Capitol Forum analysis of 2024 RealTrends data (including franchises) found combined shares of 40% in LA, 54% in Honolulu, 60% in Denver and over 80% in Manhattan and Newport Beach. It flagged "potentially illegal" concentrations (30%+ under the 2023 DOJ/FTC Merger Guidelines) in at least a dozen states — [HousingWire, Dec 26, 2025](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-market-share-analysis/). The CPC author says he could not obtain or verify the Capitol Forum report itself — [Consumer Policy Center, Apr 2026](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-expansion-report-April-2026.pdf)
- The deal "sail[ed] through DOJ review" and closed in January [snippet] — [Inman, Apr 14, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/04/14/compass-double-ending-private-exclusives-consumer-policy-center-report/)

**Compass operating data after the close**
- Q2 2026: revenue $4.3B (vs $2.06B in Q2 2025). Owned-brokerage closed transactions were 153,009 (+109.5% reported; +7.4% pro forma). Owned-brokerage agents fell from 84,187 (end Q1) to 83,184 (end Q2). Compass attributed the decline to one acquired Anywhere brand's strategy to "separate low and non-productive agents." Agent retention was 95.5%. Franchise network: 203,207 transactions, $120.0B — [HousingWire, Aug 4, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-q2-2026-revenue-anywhere/)
- Compass is rolling its "Home Platform" agent OS out to Coldwell Banker Realty, Corcoran and Sotheby's agents (4,000 in July 2026, ~50,000 targeted by end of Sept 2026), with franchise rollout planned for Q1 2027. It reported $300M in net cost synergies in year one, toward a $500M target — same source

**What analysts say it means for independents**
- T3 Sixty notes that the organic growth leaders (eXp, Real, Redfin, Side, LPT, Fathom) don't own or lease most of their offices and put that capital into agent compensation, technology or back office — [Real Estate News, Dec 22, 2025](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/12/22/trends-2026-the-brokerage-feeding-frenzy-and-market-concentration)
- NAR 2026 Member Profile: **53% of REALTORS are affiliated with an independent company**, down from the long-running ~55% figure — [NAR 2026 Member Profile (via LI Board PDF)](https://www.lirealtor.com/docs/default-source/default-document-library/research-and-industry-reports/2026-nar-member-profile.pdf); [NAR newsroom, June 25, 2026](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/experienced-realtors-anchor-the-industry-as-housing-affordability-remains-top-hurdle-new-nar-report); older ~55% figure: [NAR Franchises vs. Independents](https://www.nar.realtor/franchises-vs-independents)
- An Inman opinion column (Sept 30, 2026) argues that local independents gained share in the year Compass closed the deal, by retaining agents and building their own tech without franchise fees or investors. This is opinion, not measured research [UNCONFIRMED, 403 on fetch] — [Inman, Sept 30, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/09/30/independent-brokerage-growth-post-mega-merger/)
- Mike DelPrete on private listings: the evidence on whether they help sellers is "muddy, it's not precise — it's far from conclusive one way or the other." He expects litigation to take two to three years — [Real Estate News, June 16, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/06/16/the-private-listings-debate-leaves-more-questions-than-answers)
- The Consumer Policy Center (a consumer-advocacy think tank) expects double-ending to "rise considerably" as Anywhere is integrated. It suggests consumers "may increasingly look for agents who take fiduciary duties seriously" — [Consumer Policy Center, Apr 2026](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-expansion-report-April-2026.pdf)
- The four largest non-Compass groups (AGNT/eXp and NextHome, HomeServices, Keller Williams, Real REMAX Group) announced Open Real Estate on Oct 26, 2026. It is a shared listing-data system that "any brokerage can join as a free participating member" by signing a letter of support. No launch date has been set, and a tech vendor is to be selected in early 2027 — [Real Estate News, Oct 26, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/10/26/4-major-brokerage-firms-unveil-open-real-estate-listings-platform) (see `open_real_estate.md` for detail)

### Inferences
- For a boutique pitch, the defensible headline is: "One parent company touches about 3 in 10 US home sales and about 4 in 10 dollars of volume (2025, counting its franchise brands)." Say "counting franchise brands," because the owned-only number (~18–19% of volume) is much smaller. Competitors and journalists will notice if the two are mixed up.
- The 100,000-brokerage figure and the 53% independent affiliation figure support a "you are not alone" message to agents. Most REALTORS still work at independents.
- Consolidation is driven by acquisitions (T3), not organic consumer preference. That supports a message that bigness was bought, not earned from clients.
- Because the non-Compass majors are banding together (Open RE), the market is splitting into roughly two camps: a private-listing camp led by Compass and an "open listing" camp led by everyone else. A boutique can align publicly with the open camp at little cost.

### Gaps
- No 2025 Keller Williams or HomeServices share-of-sides figure was found in the sources reviewed (HousingWire's May 2026 piece omitted them). T3's franchise rankings would have KW, but I could not access them.
- No independent measurement of total "independent brokerage" share of transactions for 2025–2026.
- No Real Trends or DelPrete analysis focused specifically on what consolidation means for small independents.

## Q2. Florida: Compass/Anywhere brand presence in major markets and how Florida MLSs have handled private/office-exclusive listings and Clear Cooperation through 2026

### Takeaway
South Florida is a stronghold for the Compass system. In Miami-Dade, Compass ranks No. 1 and owns or controls several of the next-largest brands: Coldwell Banker, and now ONE Sotheby's, whose franchise business the Compass-owned Sotheby's franchisor bought in Sept 2026. Fort Lauderdale was flagged before the close as a "market dominance" area. Florida MLSs have taken the middle path. Stellar MLS enforces Clear Cooperation with a five-day delayed-distribution option and fines up to $15,000. Miami REALTORS MLS is cited by both WAV Group and Compass as one of the more permissive MLSs. I found nothing on BeachesMLS.

### Cited Findings
**Brand presence, South Florida**
- Miami-Dade brokerage ranking (The Real Deal, Oct 2026 issue, on-market sales): 1. Compass, 2. Douglas Elliman, 3. Coldwell Banker, 4. ONE Sotheby's International Realty, 5. Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices EWM Realty. Compass and Anywhere agents make up about half of the county's top 25 agents. Their combined brands closed **$9.5B in on-market sales** in TRD's 2025 ranking [UNCONFIRMED, snippet; 403 on fetch] — [The Real Deal, Oct 2026](https://therealdeal.com/magazine/october-2026/these-are-miamis-top-real-estate-agents-and-brokerages/)
- The Jills Zeder Group, Coldwell Banker Realty's No. 1 team nationally with $1.9B in 2025 sales, is now inside the Compass system [UNCONFIRMED, snippet] — [The Real Deal, Feb 13, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/miami/2026/02/13/miamis-jills-zeder-group-top-group-for-compass-after-merger/)
- **ONE Sotheby's International Realty** (Miami, founded 2008 by Mayi and Daniel de la Vega): its franchise business was acquired by Sotheby's International Realty (the franchisor, now under Compass) around **Sept 9, 2026**, turning it into a company-owned unit. ONE Sotheby's had **1,375 agents, $6.85B in sales and 5,257 transactions in 2025** (T3 Mega 1000). Coverage spans Florida's East Coast plus NJ offices. Daniel de la Vega stays CEO — [South Florida Agent Magazine, Sept 9, 2026](https://southfloridaagentmagazine.com/2026/09/09/sothebys-international-one-sothebys-ttr-sothebys/); [Real Estate News, Sept 9, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/09/09/sothebys-2-largest-franchises-are-now-company-owned)
- Pre-close Capitol Forum / RealTrends analysis named **Fort Lauderdale** among markets where the combined firm would have "market dominance." No percentage was published — [HousingWire, Dec 26, 2025](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-market-share-analysis/)
- An agent who works in NYC and South Florida (Heather Domi) left Compass for Douglas Elliman [UNCONFIRMED, snippet] — [The Real Deal, Jan 10, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/01/10/compass-anywhere-mega-merger-new-york-market-agents/)

**Brand presence, Tampa Bay / Orlando / Central FL**
- Coldwell Banker Realty (the company-owned brand, now Compass-owned) lists Tampa-area offices in Tampa Westshore (500 N Westshore Blvd), South Tampa, New Tampa/Westchase, Lakewood Ranch and Palm Harbor — [Coldwell Banker Realty, Tampa offices](https://www.coldwellbankerhomes.com/fl/tampa/offices/)
- Orlando-area Coldwell Banker Realty offices: Southwest Orlando (7626 W Sand Lake Rd), Orlando North/Heathrow (Lake Mary) and Winter Park (400 S Park Ave) — [Coldwell Banker Realty, Orange County offices](https://www.coldwellbankerhomes.com/fl/orange-county/offices/); [Coldwell Banker, Winter Park](https://www.coldwellbanker.com/fl/winter-park/offices/coldwell-banker-realty/oid-P00400000FDdqRN8Xm6C0DZnH2aw1OxmR3rSO6Zw)
- A Sotheby's International Realty agent in Orlando was among NAHREP's 30 top-producing Latino agents interviewed for its 2025 report — [NAHREP 2025 SHHR](https://nahrep.org/downloads/2025-state-of-hispanic-homeownership-report.pdf) (shows the Compass system is also competing for Hispanic buyers in Central FL)

**Stellar MLS (Central/West FL, Tampa Bay, Orlando)**
- Clear Cooperation 8.0 is in effect for all Stellar customers. Agents have **five business days** to enter a listing in Matrix or file a Temporary Exclusion or Office Exclusive form, and **one business day** after any public marketing — [Stellar MLS, Clear Cooperation](https://www.stellarmls.com/clearcooperation); [Stellar MLS, Delayed Distribution](https://www.stellarmls.com/DelayedDistribution)
- Office Exclusives are visible only to agents in the same office. Showing one to an internal client of the firm does not trigger MLS entry. Displaying one on the office website violates CCP. **"All forms of social media marketing"** during an exclusion period violate 8.0. Broker-to-broker communication is allowed — same sources
- As of **Sept 16, 2025**, Office Exclusive and Temporary Exclusion listings must be fully entered in Matrix and the signed authorization kept on file. NAR's Sept 30, 2025 implementation deadline also applies — [Stellar MLS, Delayed Distribution](https://www.stellarmls.com/DelayedDistribution)
- **Delayed Distribution** (Stellar's version of NAR's Multiple Listing Options for Sellers): **5 calendar days**, which cannot be extended. During the delay the listing is visible to Stellar agents and the listing office may market it as the seller authorizes. The owner signs a "Multiple Listing Options for Property Owners" waiver — same source
- Fines: a Policy 8.0 violation is a level-3 "severe" fine: **$500 first, $2,500 second, up to $15,000 after a hearing**. Fines are levied against the agent, not the broker — same source (page last updated Oct 2025)
- WAV Group (Victor Lund, July 23, 2026) lists **Stellar MLS, Miami REALTORS and Emerald Coast MLS** among MLSs with "a more practical cooperative model" that lets listings be submitted for cooperation without immediate public distribution. Lund says office exclusives in those markets have "dropped to some of the lowest numbers in the nation" (qualitative; no figures) — [WAV Group, July 23, 2026](https://www.wavgroup.com/2026/07/23/clear-cooperation-is-encouraging-office-exclusives-and-reducing-mls-cooperation/)
- I found no 2026 Compass action against Stellar specifically.

**Miami REALTORS MLS**
- Compass's Sept 2026 demand letter names **Miami Realtors MLS** among eight MLSs (with Bright, Garden State, MetroList, MLS PIN, RealTracs, West Penn and BAREIS) that, in Compass's view, allow public marketing of office exclusives. Compass says the eight cover 12 states plus DC and ~350,000 professionals. Robert Reffkin named "Miami MLS" at the Council of MLSs conference as an example — [Inman, Sept 30, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/09/30/compass-crmls-legal-fight-mls/); [Inman, Oct 1, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/10/01/reffkin-cmls-attendees-push-back/)
- Miami REALTORS is **not** among the MLSs suing Compass (CRMLS filed Oct 5, 2026; ARMLS, BridgeMLS, REcolorado, Hive, Canopy and Doorify filed Oct 22, 2026) — [The Real Deal, Oct 22, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/10/22/compass-sued-by-six-more-multiple-listing-services/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/mls-sue-compass-antitrust/)
- I could not retrieve Miami REALTORS' current rule text, so the "permits public marketing of office exclusives" description is Compass's characterization [UNCONFIRMED as to actual rules].

**National rules that frame Florida (and Wisconsin)**
- NAR's Multiple Listing Options for Sellers policy took effect March 25, 2025, with local implementation due Sept 30, 2025. It keeps CCP's one-business-day rule and lets sellers delay IDX and syndication for a locally set period with a signed seller disclosure — [NAR newsroom](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/nar-introduces-new-mls-policy-to-expand-choice-for-consumers); [NAR MLOS FAQ, Mar 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-03/multiple-listing-options-for-sellers-faq-2025-03-25.pdf)
- NAR issued guidance on July 9, 2026 tying together its office-exclusive and MLOS policies (no new rules) [snippet] — [BAM](https://nowbam.com/nar-issues-new-guidance-on-office-exclusive-and-pre-marketing-listings/). CCP has not been repealed and remains in force as of Sept 2026 — [Inman, Sept 30, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/09/30/compass-crmls-legal-fight-mls/)

**Florida demographic context (bilingual positioning)**
- Florida has the third-largest Hispanic population in the US (6.7 million). Latino buyers are among the fastest-growing segments in **Miami, Orlando and Tampa**, especially entry-level and mid-priced homes — [Florida Realtors, Mar 30, 2026](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/03/hispanic-buyers-power-florida-home-sales)
- Nationally, Hispanic households added a net 441,000 homeowners in 2025, reaching a record 10.2 million, and made up 92.6% of US household growth. Competition is most intense below about $350,000 — [NAHREP press release, Feb 10, 2026](https://nahrep.org/press-releases/2026/02/10/us-census-bureau-hispanics-post-net-gain-441000-homeowners-in-2025/); [Florida Realtors, Mar 30, 2026](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/03/hispanic-buyers-power-florida-home-sales)

### Inferences
- In South Florida, "Compass" now includes the brands consumers think of as old-line local names (Coldwell Banker, ONE Sotheby's). A boutique can truthfully say: "Several of the biggest names on Miami-Dade yard signs now report to the same parent company." Note that many Coldwell Banker and Century 21 offices elsewhere in FL are independently owned franchisees, so avoid implying every CB office is Compass-owned.
- Compass's private-listing strategy is most active where MLS rules are loosest. Miami is named as permissive, and Stellar is stricter (5-day max delay, social-media ban during exclusions, fines up to $15,000). A boutique working both Stellar and Miami markets should offer one rule regardless of MLS: "Every listing is on the MLS and syndicated unless you, the seller, choose otherwise in writing, and here is what you give up if you do."
- The Hispanic buyer pool in Miami, Orlando and Tampa is concentrated in entry-level and mid-priced homes, where off-MLS marketing helps buyers least. A bilingual boutique can pair "maximum exposure" with "service in your language" for a growing segment that luxury-focused private exclusives rarely target.

### Gaps
- No Tampa Bay, Orlando, Naples or Palm Beach market-share figures for Compass + Anywhere were found (no Tampa Bay Business Journal or South Florida Business Journal data retrieved).
- **BeachesMLS**: no 2025–2026 information found on its office-exclusive or delayed-marketing rules, or on any Compass dispute. Check the BeachesMLS rules directly.
- Miami REALTORS MLS rule text was not retrieved. Its delay period and fine schedule are unknown.
- No Compass Florida agent count or Florida office count was found.
- Whether any Florida metro is on Compass's "30 markets" target list is not public in the sources found.
- No count of office-exclusive listings in any Florida MLS.

## Q3. Wisconsin: Compass/Anywhere presence and how Wisconsin MLSs and the Wisconsin REALTORS Association have responded to private listings

### Takeaway
Compass's own Wisconsin footprint is small and recent. It entered Milwaukee in Aug 2021 with 16 agents and added Lake Geneva (via Keefe Real Estate) and Lake Country. The merger adds Coldwell Banker Realty (with a Milwaukee office) and, through the earlier @properties/Christie's purchase, a Christie's affiliate presence in Madison and Lake Geneva. The biggest local names, Shorewest (family-owned since 1946) and First Weber, did not appear in any Compass deal coverage. Wisconsin MLSs appear to follow NAR policy closely: Metro MLS enforces CCP and allows MLS-member-only listings, and SCWMLS's handbook cites CCP. WRA has framed the rules as "opportunities" and published a state MLOS addendum (Addendum LM). No Wisconsin MLS appears in Compass's demand letter, WAV's "flexible" list, or the MLS lawsuits.

### Cited Findings
**Compass and Compass-family brands in Wisconsin**
- Compass entered Wisconsin in **August 2021**, opening in Milwaukee with 16 agents who represented more than $110M in 2020 volume (including The Sewart Group) — [Compass press release, "Compass Expands to Wisconsin"](https://investors.compass.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/161/compass-expands-to-wisconsin)
- By Nov 5, 2021: 44 agents, 8 unlicensed team members and 14 staff. **Keefe Real Estate (Lake Geneva)** joined Compass, keeping its leadership and agents, and the **JJ Hausmann team** founded Compass's Lake Country presence — [BizTimes Milwaukee, Nov 5, 2021](https://biztimes.com/compass-adds-real-estate-agents-in-lake-country-lake-geneva) (background, pre-window)
- 2026 Metro MLS listing pages show active Compass offices: "Compass RE WI-Northshore," "Compass Wisconsin-Lake Geneva" and "Compass-Tosa." ## Q4. Consumer and agent sentiment: national brand vs. local/independent, trust and local expertise, agent satisfaction and turnover, and why agents leave big brokerages

### Takeaway
Consumers choose **agents, not brands**. In NAR's 2025 data, about two-thirds of sellers used an agent who was referred to them or had served them before, and most interviewed only one agent. Agents remain the most trusted information source. The only direct "local vs national brand" consumer survey is old and sponsored (2017, LeadingRE: 88% prefer a well-known local company). On the agent side, **leadership and culture (40%) outrank splits (23%)** as the reason agents switch. Before the close, more than half of Anywhere agents said they would leave or consider leaving if Compass bought Anywhere, and about 70% said private listing networks are not in sellers' best interest (Zillow survey; Zillow is an interested party).

### Cited Findings
**Consumers (NAR 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers; survey period July 2024–June 2025; released Nov 4, 2025)**
- 88% of buyers bought through an agent. **91% of sellers used an agent**, tying the record. FSBO fell to 5%, an all-time low, and FSBO homes sold at a median $360,000 vs $425,000 for agent-assisted sales — [NAR, Nov 4, 2025](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/nar-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-sellers-reveals-market-extremes)
- Agents were the **most trusted and most used information source**, ahead of online listings. 76% of first-time buyers credited their agent with helping them understand the process. Sellers' top selection priorities were marketing the home, pricing it competitively and selling within a set timeframe — same source
- First-time buyers fell to 21% of buyers, a record low, at a record median age of 40. Median seller age is 64 — same source
- How buyers found agents: ~43% through a friend, neighbor or relative (49% of first-time buyers, 41% of repeat buyers) and ~18% used an agent they had worked with before. **66% of sellers** used a referred or previously used agent. **81% of sellers**, 67% of first-time buyers and 76% of repeat buyers interviewed only one agent (secondary summaries of the NAR report; figures vary slightly, 40–43% for referrals, across write-ups) — [Virginia REALTORS, Dec 8, 2025](https://virginiarealtors.org/2025/12/08/key-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers/); [BAM](https://nowbam.com/how-home-buyers-and-sellers-find-their-agents-in-2025/); [NAR 2025 Profile highlights PDF](https://www.rirealtors.org/clientuploads/documents/NAR/Homebuyers_and_sellers_trend_2025.pdf)
- ~91% of buyers would use their agent again or recommend them [secondary; UNCONFIRMED against NAR original] — [BAM](https://nowbam.com/88-of-home-buyers-still-rely-on-agents-nar-2025-report-finds/)

**Local vs national brand (consumer)**
- The most direct survey is a **2017 LeadingRE-commissioned study**: more than 88% preferred an agent with a "well-known, local real estate company," vs 12% preferring a national franchise. It is dated and commissioned by an independents' network — [RISMedia, June 1, 2017](https://www.rismedia.com/2017/06/01/survey-homeowners-show-preference-independent-real-estate-brands/)
- Agent-side proxy: in a 2025 Real Brokerage survey of 260+ agents, only **6%** named brand recognition as a top-three factor in choosing their current company [snippet; UNCONFIRMED detail] — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/real-brokerage-agent-survey-homebuyers-gain-leverage-real-estate-market-slows/)

**Agents: who they are and where they work (NAR 2026 Member Profile, released June 25, 2026; 2025 data)**
- **53% of REALTORS are affiliated with an independent company.** 86% are independent contractors. Median tenure at their current firm is 6 years — [NAR 2026 Member Profile PDF](https://www.lirealtor.com/docs/default-source/default-document-library/research-and-industry-reports/2026-nar-member-profile.pdf); [NAR newsroom](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/experienced-realtors-anchor-the-industry-as-housing-affordability-remains-top-hurdle-new-nar-report)
- Median gross income was $59,200 (2025). The typical REALTOR had 9 transaction sides, and the typical team 31. **28% of business came from repeat clients and 22% from past-client referrals.** 75% are "very certain" to stay active for two more years. Typical age is 57 — [NAR Economists' Outlook, Aug 19, 2026](https://www.nar.realtor/news/economists-outlook/the-feature-sheet-2026-member-profile)

**Why agents move**
- **1000Watt agent survey (2025)**: top reason for changing brokerages was **better leadership/culture (40%)**, then **better split/compensation (23%)**. Marketing support (7%), technology (6%), training (2%) and help navigating industry change (2%) together came to only 17% — [Recruiting Insight, May 27, 2025](https://recruitinginsight.net/2025/05/27/the-real-reason-agents-change-brokerages/)
- **Real Brokerage monthly agent survey (June 2026; 153 US/Canadian agents; company-sponsored)**: if moving today, economics 25%, lead generation 19%, brokerage support 14% — [Yahoo Finance / Real](https://finance.yahoo.com/real-estate/articles/real-monthly-agent-survey-competitive-130000014.html); [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/REAX/real-s-monthly-agent-survey-competitive-economics-remain-the-top-fcsmzy9zzcx0.html)
- Turnover rates conflict across methods: 13% of active agents changed brokerages in 2024 (Recruiting Insight/BoldTrail) vs 6.8% annual turnover in 2025, up from 6.0% (later Recruiting Insight analysis). Agents are nearly **twice as likely to move when an office loses 20%+ of its agents** to competitors — [HousingWire (BoldTrail/Recruiting Insight)](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/real-estate-agent-movement-brokerage-leadership-recruiting-insight-boldtrail/); [HousingWire, agent migration 2025](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/agent-migration-2025-trends/) [figures via search summary; UNCONFIRMED in detail]

**Merger-specific agent sentiment and behavior**
- **Zillow survey of Anywhere agents (fielded late Oct 2025; released Nov 6, 2025; sample size not disclosed)**: 18% "definitely will" and 35% "might consider" leaving within a year if Compass acquired Anywhere (53% total). 23% expected a negative impact on their business. About **70% said a private listing network is not in a seller's best interest**. 55% named the brokerage (34%) or agent (20%) as the main beneficiary, vs sellers (16%) and buyers (12%). 98% cited at least one negative impact, including "competitive disadvantages for smaller brokerages and independent agents." **Caveat: Zillow is Compass's litigation opponent** — [Zillow / PR Newswire, Nov 6, 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/half-of-anywhere-real-estate-agents-say-they-would-consider-leaving-if-compass-acquisition-moves-forward-302607784.html)
- Actual post-close movement looks smaller than the stated intent: Compass owned-brokerage agents fell by a net 1,003 in Q2 2026 (84,187 → 83,184), with 95.5% retention, and Compass says the drop was a deliberate cut of low producers — [HousingWire, Aug 4, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-q2-2026-revenue-anywhere/). A separate figure of 3,819 separations vs 2,816 additions in Q2 appeared in a search summary but was not in the HousingWire article [UNCONFIRMED]
- Layoffs hit Compass and Anywhere staff about six weeks after the close — [Inman, Feb 26, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/02/26/with-merger-complete-layoffs-come-for-anywhere-and-compass/); [The Real Deal, Mar 6, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/03/06/compass-post-merger-layoffs-are-here/)
- Franchise defection: the largest Coldwell Banker franchise in NYC (Coldwell Banker Reliable, ~135 agents) disaffiliated in April 2026, relaunched as independent **MYNY**, and joined **LeadingRE**. Its principal: "When there's a lot of sameness across brands, it becomes difficult to differentiate." Coldwell Banker's CEO has said the CB brand "will never be swapped out for Compass" — [The Real Deal, Apr 23, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/04/23/why-top-coldwell-banker-franchise-went-independent/) (quotes via search summary)
- Brand folding in practice: Coldwell Banker Warburg agents were told they would operate as "Warburg at Compass" — [The Real Deal, July 24, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/07/24/nyc-brokerage-coldwell-banker-warburg-joins-compass/). The Real Deal also reported that consolidation "cooled agent moves" in New York (headline only; detail not retrieved) — [The Real Deal, July 29, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/07/29/how-compass-consolidation-cooled-new-york-agent-moves/)

**Consumer-protection evidence on private listings (useful for client messaging)**
- A Compass internal slide surfaced in litigation said pre-marketing "results in a consistently higher percentage of [transactions] where Compass is on both sides." It showed off-market sales **double-ending 72% more often** than on-market sales. "Old Compass" double-end rate in Washington DC was **41%** (CPC's 1,000-sale sample). Compass also offers agents a **10% internal referral fee** for referring clients to other Compass agents — [Consumer Policy Center, Apr 2026](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-expansion-report-April-2026.pdf)
- DelPrete's team posed as buyer agents seeking access to Compass Private Exclusives and reported that **90%** were "ghosted" or "refused" (cited by CPC from a court exhibit) — [Consumer Policy Center, Apr 2026](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-expansion-report-April-2026.pdf)
- Competing price claims: a Compass study (Apr 2025–Mar 2026, 70,000+ closings) claims a **4.6% premium** for homes that started as Private Exclusive or Coming Soon. Independent research found a smaller 1.7% off-MLS premium, and a Bright MLS/Drexel study found the opposite. DelPrete calls the evidence "muddy" — [RISMedia, July 24, 2026](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/07/24/compass-releases-study-private-listings-larger-premium/); [Real Estate News, June 16, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/06/16/the-private-listings-debate-leaves-more-questions-than-answers); [The Real Deal, Sept 24, 2026 ("The private listing premium that never was")](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/09/24/the-private-listing-premium-that-never-was/)
- Scale of office exclusives: DelPrete data shows office exclusives at about **5% of new listings** nationally, peaking at 6.6% in spring 2025 [UNCONFIRMED, snippet]. Bright MLS reports nearly 8% in its footprint [snippet, date unclear] — [The Real Deal, Sept 24, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/09/24/the-private-listing-premium-that-never-was/); [BAM / Bright MLS tag](https://nowbam.com/tag/bright-mls/)

### Inferences
- The strongest current evidence for a boutique is behavioral: clients pick a person through referrals and rarely shop around. Brand matters little to consumers. Only 6% of agents in one survey cited brand among their top-three reasons, and no recent data shows consumers prefer national brands.
- For agent recruiting, the 1000Watt data says to lead with leadership, culture and broker access, then economics. Tech and marketing rarely move agents, so a boutique doesn't need to match Compass's Home Platform feature for feature.
- Stated intent to leave (53%) far exceeded measured departures (about 1% net per quarter at Compass-owned brokerages). Expect slow, steady attrition and team-level breaks, not a flood. Recruit patiently, the "they'll be calling us" approach (see Q5).
- For client messaging, the double-ending data plus the "ghosting" test support a fiduciary pitch: "With a private listing, the brokerage may be the main beneficiary. With us, your home goes to every buyer's agent." Cite neutral sources (CPC, NAR) over Zillow's survey, since Zillow is a litigant.

- **J.D. Power**: no 2025 or 2026 Home Buyer/Seller Satisfaction Study was found (the latest results surfaced were years old). The study may have been discontinued [UNCONFIRMED].
- No recent (2025–2026), neutral consumer survey directly comparing preference for local independents vs national brands.
- NAR's 2025 Profile "most important factor in choosing an agent" (e.g., honesty/trustworthiness, local knowledge) was not retrieved from the primary report.
- No agent-satisfaction comparison between large brokerages and boutiques (e.g., NPS by brokerage size) was found.
- No systematic survey of why agents actually left Compass/Anywhere after the close. Evidence is anecdotal (MYNY, Domi).
- No data on Spanish-speaking or bilingual agents' share in FL or WI, or on Hispanic consumers' preference for language-concordant agents.

## Q5. Concrete strategies independents and boutiques are using in 2025–2026 to compete

### Takeaway
Independents compete on five things: (1) **open-listing positioning**, now backed by the Open Real Estate coalition, which any brokerage can join free; (2) **network affiliation** without franchise loss of identity (LeadingRE, Top Agent Network); (3) **culture, coaching and broker access** as the recruiting story ("do you really want to be in the middle of a mall or a big box store"); (4) **patient recruiting** of agents disrupted by integration and layoffs; (5) **lean overhead and community ties**. I found no data showing formal "full MLS exposure" pledges spreading among boutiques specifically, but MLS rules and Open RE's principles provide ready-made language.

### Cited Findings
**Open-listing coalition**
- **Open Real Estate** (announced Oct 26, 2026): founded by AGNT (eXp and NextHome), HomeServices of America, Keller Williams and Real REMAX Group. Early participants include Brown Harris Stevens, Lamacchia Realty, Samson Properties, Windermere and Iron Valley. **Any brokerage can join free by signing the open letter of support.** Listings are entered once and syndicated to MLSs and portals, alongside MLSs rather than replacing them. Principles: expand buyer opportunity, informed seller decisions, data integrity, fair competition. The founders call it "not a walled garden." No launch date has been set, with vendor selection in early 2027 — [Real Estate News, Oct 26, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/10/26/4-major-brokerage-firms-unveil-open-real-estate-listings-platform); [Inman, Oct 26, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/10/26/keller-williams-agnt-real-remax-open-real-estate/)
- Iron Valley CEO Rob Cleapor: the industry should compete "on service, expertise, and results." A Compass spokesperson said the initiative "returns power to the creator of the listing." Critics note Open RE's rules also include a participant-only (non-public) option — [Real Estate News, Oct 26, 2026](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/10/26/4-major-brokerage-firms-unveil-open-real-estate-listings-platform); [WRE News](https://wrenews.com/open-real-estate-national-listing-network-participant-only-2026/)

**Networks of independents**
- **LeadingRE**: MYNY (ex-Coldwell Banker franchise, ~135 agents) joined it in April 2026 for relocation, referrals and market intelligence — [The Real Deal, Apr 23, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/04/23/why-top-coldwell-banker-franchise-went-independent/). At a March 2026 LeadingRE panel, independent leaders cited "lean overhead, real coaching, authentic community ties, and smart (not performative) tech adoption" as their playbook [secondary blog summarizing HousingWire; UNCONFIRMED] — [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/when-giants-like-compass-and-anywhere-merge-the-agile-brokers-and-agents-win/)
- **Top Agent Network (TAN)**: analyst Rob Hahn suggests smaller firms use TAN rather than try to build their own private network to match Compass's. City Real Estate (No. 1 in San Francisco per RealTrends) has TAN-member agents — [HousingWire, Feb 3, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-anywhere-agent-strategies/)

**Recruiting and positioning tactics (named brokers)**
- Douglas Elliman CEO Michael Liebowitz: "do you really want to be in the middle of a mall or a big box store." He is waiting for integration cuts rather than poaching content agents ("they'll be calling us") — [HousingWire, Feb 3, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/compass-anywhere-agent-strategies/)
- City Real Estate (Shalini Sadda): recruits on a "culture of learning and growth" plus an in-house marketing team and support staff — same source
- Steve Murray (RTC Consulting): splits attract agents, but tight margins limit how far a firm can go. He expects Compass to keep recruiting hard and advises rivals to watch for team "breakage" during integration, since Compass agents no longer have exclusive tech — same source
- Chip Stella (Rutledge Properties): questions whether Compass's high splits and sign-on bonuses continue once so many agents sit inside one firm — same source
- Brown Harris Stevens CEO Bess Freedman: "Not everyone wants a big company" [snippet] — [The Real Deal, Jan 10, 2026](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/01/10/compass-anywhere-mega-merger-new-york-market-agents/)
- An Inman column (May 29, 2026) argues independents win when leaders "own every decision," meaning faster, local decision-making (headline only; not retrieved) — [Inman, May 29, 2026](https://www.inman.com/2026/05/29/independent-brokerage-leadership-decision-making/)

**Seller-exposure promise (the "full MLS" pitch)**
- Under NAR rules, a seller who chooses delayed marketing or an office exclusive must sign a disclosure acknowledging the benefits of public marketing they give up — [NAR MLOS FAQ](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-03/multiple-listing-options-for-sellers-faq-2025-03-25.pdf); Florida form example: Stellar's "Multiple Listing Options for Property Owners" — [Stellar MLS](https://www.stellarmls.com/DelayedDistribution); Wisconsin form: WRA Addendum LM — [WRA, Aug 2025](https://www.wra.org/WREM/Aug25/MLOS/)
- CPC's consumer guidance: sellers may come to see that private listing "limits its exposure to potential purchasers and also may lengthen the time to sell." Buyers may favor portals that show days on market and price changes, which private listings increasingly omit — [Consumer Policy Center, Apr 2026](https://consumerpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/Compass-expansion-report-April-2026.pdf)
- About 98% of homes still trade through the MLS (WAV Group) — [WAV Group, July 23, 2026](https://www.wavgroup.com/2026/07/23/clear-cooperation-is-encouraging-office-exclusives-and-reducing-mls-cooperation/)

**Bilingual / community niche**
- Hispanic households drove 92.6% of US household growth in 2025, and Latino buyers are among the fastest-growing segments in Miami, Orlando and Tampa, mostly entry-level and mid-priced — [NAHREP, Feb 10, 2026](https://nahrep.org/press-releases/2026/02/10/us-census-bureau-hispanics-post-net-gain-441000-homeowners-in-2025/); [Florida Realtors, Mar 30, 2026](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/03/hispanic-buyers-power-florida-home-sales)
- NAHREP's 2025 report draws on interviews with 30 top-producing Latino agents and loan officers. They describe longer timelines, more price cuts and more concessions, with the fiercest competition under ~$350,000 — [NAHREP 2025 SHHR](https://nahrep.org/downloads/2025-state-of-hispanic-homeownership-report.pdf); [GAAR summary](https://www.gaar.com/blog/article/nahreps-2025-state-of-hispanic-homeownership-report)

### Inferences
- **Cheapest strong move:** sign Open Real Estate's free letter of support and say so in listing presentations ("we stand with the open-listing coalition alongside Keller Williams, eXp, RE/MAX and HomeServices"). It puts a small firm next to national names without a franchise fee. Watch the participant-only option before calling it "fully public."
- **Written seller-exposure promise:** "Your home goes on Stellar/Miami/Metro MLS/SCWMLS and is syndicated everywhere within one business day unless you choose otherwise in writing." Back it with the NAR-required disclosure form and the CPC double-ending data. This works the same in Florida and Wisconsin because both states' MLSs keep CCP.
- **Network for scale without losing identity:** LeadingRE (relocation and referrals, the MYNY model) and/or TAN (agent-to-agent off-market intel) answer the "but they have a bigger network" objection.
- **Agent recruiting order:** culture and broker access (40% reason) → fair, transparent economics (23–25%) → lead flow (19%). Target Coldwell Banker Realty, Century 21 and Sotheby's agents in FL and WI as Home Platform and other integration changes reach them (franchise rollout Q1 2027). A practical window: Q4 2026 to mid-2027.
- **Bilingual niche:** the fastest-growing buyer segment (Hispanic) is concentrated in the price bands where private exclusives matter least and MLS exposure matters most. "Exposición total + servicio en español" pairs both advantages.

### Gaps
- No data on how many independents have adopted formal "transparency pledges" or "always-MLS" guarantees, or on whether such pledges change win rates.
- No Florida- or Wisconsin-specific examples of boutiques using these strategies in 2025–2026 were found.
- LeadingRE membership counts, fees and 2026 growth were not retrieved.
- No measured evidence that independents gained share post-merger (the Inman Sept 30, 2026 claim is opinion and could not be fetched).
