# Compass, Inc. (NYSE: COMP): Corporate Moves and Business Health, Jan 2025 – Oct 26, 2026

Research date: October 26, 2026. Scope: US only. Every item is dated. **[CONFIRMED]** = primary source (SEC filing, company release) or multiple reputable trade/business outlets agree. **[REPORTED]** = one outlet or secondary source, not independently verified. **[ALLEGATION]** = claims in lawsuits or plaintiff-firm notices. **[OPINION]** = analyst or commentator view. **[UNVERIFIED]** = could not confirm.

A note on terms: Compass's agent metrics changed after the merger. Through Q4 2025 Compass reported **"principal agents"** (~21,000, lead agents only). From Q1 2026 it reports **"total Brokerage agents"** (~83,000–84,000, agents at company-owned brokerages including Coldwell Banker Realty, Corcoran, Sotheby's owned offices, @properties, etc.). The **~340,000** figure includes franchise agents (Century 21, Coldwell Banker affiliates, ERA, BHGRE, and others). These three numbers measure different things and should not be compared directly.

## 1. The Anywhere Real Estate acquisition: terms, regulatory review, closing, and aftermath

### Takeaway
Compass announced an all-stock deal for Anywhere on Sept 22, 2025 (~$1.6B equity value, ~$10B combined enterprise value including debt). It closed on Jan 9, 2026, much earlier than the "second half of 2026" first guided, after the DOJ let the HSR waiting period lapse without an extended "Second Request" review. Since closing, Compass has cut costs aggressively ($330M Year-1 net synergies actioned; 110 WARN-notice layoffs at Anywhere's NJ HQ), replaced Anywhere's top leadership, folded at least one Coldwell Banker brokerage into the Compass/Christie's banners, and rolled its tech platform into the owned brands. The regulatory story is not over. The New York AG opened an antitrust probe (June 2026), members of Congress have questioned the DOJ's handling, and a securities class action filed Oct 7, 2026 alleges Compass hid a lobbying effort to avoid DOJ review.

### Inferences
- The early close (Jan 2026 vs. guided 2H 2026) and the lack of a DOJ Second Request are the root of most current legal and political risk: the NY AG probe, congressional letters, and the Oct 2026 securities suit. Because the HSR clearance is not a permanent safe harbor, divestiture risk in concentrated markets such as Manhattan cannot be ruled out. This is an inference, not a reported likelihood.
- The integration so far looks like a classic cost-out: HQ layoffs, executive turnover at the franchise brands, and pruning of low-producing agents. The visible brand consolidation (Warburg → Compass → Christie's NYC) suggests Compass is willing to re-flag acquired company-owned offices. No wholesale rebrand of Coldwell Banker, Century 21, Sotheby's, or Corcoran has been announced.
- The "preserve the franchise brands" pledge has held publicly. Departures of franchise-side executives (CB Affiliates president, franchise growth VP, ERA head) and at least one major franchisee leaving (MYNY) are early signs of franchise-network friction worth watching.

## 2. Other acquisitions and recruiting moves (2025–2026); national market share and agent headcount

### Takeaway
Before Anywhere, Compass closed the $444M @properties/Christie's International Real Estate deal (Jan 13, 2025) and bought several regional independents in 2025 (Washington Fine Properties, PorchLight, Cottingham Chalk). After Anywhere it became by far the largest US brokerage group. RealTrends' 2025 data shows Compass alone with ~11% of verified sales volume and ~5.9% of verified transaction sides. Combined with Anywhere's brands, the group's share of the RealTrends-verified universe is roughly 29% of sides and 39% of volume. Company-owned brokerage agents number ~83,000 (Q2 2026), and ~340,000 agents including franchises.

### Inferences
- For a boutique brokerage owner's team meeting, the framing that fits the evidence:
  - Compass is now the dominant US brokerage group by volume.
  - It is operationally strong: record Q2, profitable, cost synergies ahead of plan.
  - It is under pressure from regulators, shareholders, and the MLS establishment over how it got the deal approved and over its private-listing strategy.
  - The fight that most directly affects local agents is the MLS litigation. Its outcome will shape whether office exclusives and private listings spread or are curbed in many markets.
- The NWMLS settlement and the Chicago/Zillow ruling give Compass some momentum. The Feb 2026 Zillow injunction loss and the six-MLS coalition suit show strong institutional resistance. Outcomes are uncertain, and no court has ruled on the core antitrust questions in the new suits.
