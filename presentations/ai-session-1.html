The formula

Role · Task · Context · Examples · Format

¡Pa’ fuera la vaguedad! Five pieces. Zero guessing.

1
Role
Who should Claude be?
“You’re my bilingual real estate assistant.”
Skip it and…A generic voice.
2
Task
What do you need done?
“Write a follow-up text to a buyer who saw my open house.”
Skip it and…Claude guesses the job.
3
Context
What should it know?
“I’m Maria in Kissimmee. They liked the kitchen, worried about price.”
Skip it and…Claude invents the details.
4
Examples
What does good look like?
“My style: ‘Hi Carlos! Loved meeting you.’”
Skip it and…It sounds like everyone.
5
Format
How should it come back?
“3 versions, under 40 words, English then Spanish.”
Skip it and…A wall of text.

Tone matters

Calm and clear beats ALL CAPS

Con calma y claridad. Ask for what you want, don’t shout what you don’t.

Shouting
YOU MUST WRITE A POST!!! NEVER USE FLUFF!!! ALWAYS BE SHORT!!! DO NOT FORGET!!!
✗ Stiff, nervous, one-size-fits-all.
Calm and specific
Please write one Instagram caption for my open house on Saturday. Warm and direct, like talking to a neighbor. Here’s an example of the tone I like: “Come see it, ask me anything.”
✓ Relaxed, specific, usable.
Say what good looks like
“Warm, under 50 words.”
Show an example
“Here’s a post I loved.”
Ask me questions first
“Ask me 3 questions before you write.”
Use [placeholders]
“[price], [address]: I’ll fill these in.”

# My market

[Neighborhoods, price range, buyers or sellers].

# My voice and tone

Warm, direct, no hype. English with Spanish touches.

# Rules

Fair Housing safe language. Never invent numbers or stats. Brokerage name in every ad. Write in English and Spanish.

# My folders

01-Clients to 06-Archive. Save work where it belongs.

# How I like answers

Short. Bullets first. Ask me before guessing.
