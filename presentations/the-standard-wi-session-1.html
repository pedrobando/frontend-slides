The File Outlives the Deal

“

“A firm shall retain for at least 2 years… exact and complete copies of all listing contracts, agency agreements, offers to purchase, … and other documents or correspondence utilized, received or prepared in connection with any transaction. The retention period shall run from the date of closing of the transaction or, if the transaction has not been consummated, from the date the listing contract or the agency agreement is terminated.”

— WIS. ADMIN. CODE REEB 15.04(1)
En cristiano: If a client ever signed a listing contract or agency agreement, the file stays two years — from closing, or from termination if the deal never closed.

Before, Not After

“

“No firm… may negotiate on behalf of a party who is not the firm’s client unless the firm… provides to the party a copy of the… written disclosure statement.” — the Disclosure to Customers. “…a firm shall provide to a client a copy of the… written disclosure statement not later than the time the firm enters into an agency agreement with the client.” — the Disclosure to Clients.

— Wis. Stat. §452.135(1)(a) & (2)(a)
En cristiano: Customers get the disclosure before you negotiate for them. Clients get it no later than the moment they sign the agency agreement.

Act 2 · §452.133–.135

Three Relationships. One Piece of Paper.

Wisconsin gives you three ways to work with a buyer or seller. If nothing is in writing — they’re a customer.

No Written Agreement? This Is Them
Customer
Everyone you serve — owed all seven §452.133(1) duties. But you may NOT negotiate on their behalf.
§452.134(1)(b): a firm may not negotiate on behalf of a party unless that party is the firm’s client.
Written Agency Agreement
Client
The written agreement makes them a client — adds the loyalty tier + the duty to negotiate for them.
§452.01(1m): a written agreement between a firm and a client authorizing brokerage services.
Written Consent — Checkbox in the Form
Multiple Representation
Both sides are the firm’s clients — with designated agency two agents advocate; without it, the firm stays neutral.
§452.134(2)&(3): all clients’ consent in writing; designated agency needs its own separate written consent.
In Wisconsin, the paper makes the client. No writing — no client. But never zero duties.

When the Law Demands Paper

Two legal requirements. Zero drafting — the language lives in our forms.

Customer
Disclosure to Customers BEFORE you negotiate for them.
§452.135(1)
Client
A written agency agreement — that’s what makes a client — with Disclosure to Clients at signing.
§452.01(1m) · §240.10 · §452.135(2)
You never draft this language — the state wrote it into the approved forms (REEB 16.04(1) makes them mandatory):
📄Residential Listing Contract (disclosure + consent inside)
📄Buyer Agency Agreement (disclosure + consent inside)
📄Disclosure to Customers (standalone)
When would the consent checkbox matter?
Your buyer-client falls for your firm’s own listing. Both clients consent in writing — with designated agency, two agents each advocate for their own client; without it, the firm goes strictly neutral. It’s a three-way checkbox printed right in the form.
A phone call is not consent. A text that says “ok” is not consent. Signed or initialed. On file.
