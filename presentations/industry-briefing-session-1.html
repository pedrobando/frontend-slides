Act 2 · Compensation

The seller offers. The offer documents it.

New WB-1 language. State law prohibits a firm from paying compensation to another firm, referral fees aside. If the buyer’s firm gets paid by the other side, the seller pays it.
Step 1 · WB-1 listing
The seller decides
Seller does or does not offer compensation to another firm. Left blank, the answer is does not.
Step 2 · WB-11 offer
The offer documents it
New section. Check one: buyer agent compensation for a buyer client, or the seller’s offer of compensation for a buyer customer. Write the % or $.
Step 3 · Closing
The seller pays the buyer’s firm
Paid at closing. It doesn’t create an agency relationship with the seller.
WB-36 · Buyer agencyYour firm can ask the seller to pay, but only with every party’s written consent first. The buyer pays the rest, plus anything the firm doesn’t collect.
On every formNo standard commission. Not set by law. Fully negotiable. The same goes for offers of compensation.

Wis. Stat. § 452.19: seller compensation to another firm is documented in the offer to purchase (1 to 4 unit residential) · WRA educational redlines

Re-prorate taxes after closing

Taxes get re-prorated on the actual tax bill. The buyer forwards it within 5 days. The parties settle within 30 days. Not the firms’ job.

Part of the new WB-11

Disclose a referral fee when you send or receive a referral. Wisconsin law doesn’t require it. It’s there for transparency.

Act 4 · Your kit

The kitchen-table answer, in 60 seconds

Your sellerThe Compass agent said they can sell it privately first. Should we do that?
You · don’t trash Compass. Ask three questions.

Good question. Before you decide, ask any agent these three things:

1
How many agents will see my home?
Private means one company’s agents and their buyers.
2
Who will represent the buyer?
Off-market sales end with one firm on both sides 72% more often.
3
What won’t buyers see?
Coming Soon hides days on market and price cuts.

With us, everyone sees it on day one. If you still want private, we’ll do it right, in writing. It’s your call.
