Rent vs. Own

Why ownership wins
long-term.

Every monthly rent check pays off someone else's mortgage. Owning your home flips the script: each payment builds something you keep.

Build Real Equity
Each mortgage payment builds ownership in an asset you control. After 5 years, you may have tens of thousands in equity instead of a stack of rent receipts.
Lock In Your Payment
A fixed-rate mortgage means your principal & interest payment stays the same for 30 years — even when rents in your area keep climbing.
Tax Benefits
Mortgage interest and property taxes can be deductible. Talk to a CPA — many buyers see real savings come April.
Make It Yours
Paint the walls. Replace the floors. Plant the garden. No landlord, no security-deposit fear, no permission slips.
Stability for Family
Same school district. Same neighbors. Same routine. Owning brings predictability that renting rarely offers.
Generational Wealth
A home is the #1 way most families build long-term wealth that can be passed down. You're building a legacy.
$1,200
Median U.S. rent. Twelve years of that is $172,800 with nothing to show. Twelve years of mortgage payments builds an asset.

Mindset

Decide on purpose — not someday.

Before paper work, before pre-approval, before showings — the first step is internal. Renters think in months. Owners think in years. Switching that lens is what makes everything else possible.

Three shifts to make today
Rent isn't easy — it's expensive. You're building someone else's wealth.
Trade 'someday' for a 12–24 month window. Vague goals = vague results.
You don't do this alone. You hire a team: agent, lender, title, inspector.
Bust the myths
You don't need 20% down. Conventional starts at 3%, FHA 3.5%, VA/USDA 0%.
Your credit doesn't have to be perfect. FHA can approve down to 580.
'I'll wait for prices to drop' usually loses to 'rent keeps going up.'
Your why
Stability for your family. A yard. A wall to paint. Equity instead of receipts.
Write it down. When the process gets bumpy (it will), your why carries you.
Pro Tip
Waiting feels safe — the math usually says it isn't. Two years of paying rent vs. building equity can be a $50K+ swing.

2
Credit

Move your score on purpose.

Your credit score decides whether you qualify, which programs you qualify for, and the rate you'll pay. Good news: it's a number you can move — most buyers lift their score 20–60 points in 90 days.

What lenders look for
580+ FHA loan minimum
620+ Conventional minimum
680+ Better rates, more options
740+ Best-tier rates, lowest PMI
Do this
Pay every bill on time — set autopay on the minimum, always.
Keep balances under 30% of each card's limit; under 10% is even better.
Dispute legitimate errors on your report.
Don't do this
Don't close old credit cards — length of history matters.
Don't open new credit lines 6 months before applying.
Don't co-sign for anyone until you've closed.
Pro Tip
Ask each card for a credit-limit increase (no hard pull). Same balance + higher limit = lower utilization = higher score. Costs nothing.

3
Budget & Save

Know your numbers before a lender does.

The biggest reason first-time buyers get spooked at the closing table is they don't know the real costs going in. Get clear on the four buckets of home buying cash and build a payment you actually want to live with.

The four cash buckets
Down payment — 0% to 20% of price (loan-dependent).
Closing costs — typically 2–5% of purchase price.
Earnest money — 1–3% at offer, applied to closing.
Move-in reserves — 1–3 months PITI + setup.
Your real monthly cost (PITI)
P — Principal (the wealth-building part)
I — Interest
T — Taxes (varies by city)
I — Insurance + PMI/MIP (if under 20% down)
Saving rhythm
Open a separate Home Fund (high-yield online savings).
Automate payday transfers — even $300 / 2 weeks = $7,800 / year.
Stack windfalls 100% — tax refunds, bonuses, side income.
Pro Tip
Aim to keep total PITI under 28% of your gross monthly income. Lenders qualify you on what you CAN pay. You should buy what you WANT to pay.

4
Pre-Approval

Lock in your buying power.

Pre-approval is a lender's written statement that they're willing to loan you a specific amount. No serious offer goes out without one. Get quotes from 2–3 lenders inside a 7-day window so credit bureaus treat them as a single inquiry.

Documents to gather
2 years of W-2s (or 1099s if self-employed)
30 days of recent paystubs
2 months of bank statements (all accounts)
2 years of federal tax returns
Photo ID + landlord contact info
Loan types — at a glance
Conventional 3–20% down, 620+ score
FHA 3.5% down, 580+ score
VA 0% down, military/veterans
USDA 0% down, eligible areas
What you'll learn
Your max price — and your comfortable price.
Estimated monthly payment, taxes, and insurance.
Estimated cash to close.
Which programs and DPA you qualify for.
Pro Tip
You marry the home, you date the rate. A higher rate today can be refinanced later — but the home you love won't wait.

Getting Pre-Approved

Pre-approval is a lender's written statement that they're willing to loan you a certain amount, based on your income, credit, and assets. No serious offer goes out without one.

Documents to gather before you call a lender
✓2 years of W-2s (or 1099s if self-employed)
✓30 days of recent paystubs
✓2 months of bank statements (all pages, all accounts)
✓2 years of federal tax returns
✓Photo ID — driver's license or passport
✓Asset documentation — retirement, brokerage, gift letters
✓Landlord contact info — for proof of rent payments
✓For self-employed: profit/loss statements & business returns
How long does it take? Submitting documents takes 30 minutes. A real pre-approval letter typically comes back in 24–72 hours. Verbal "pre-qualifications" are not the same thing — insist on pre-approval.
What you'll learn
✓Your maximum purchase price — and your comfortable purchase price
✓The loan programs you qualify for
✓Your estimated monthly payment, including taxes & insurance
✓Estimated cash to close

Loan Types

Find the right fit

Most first-time buyers fall into one of four loan buckets. Each has trade-offs — the right answer depends on your credit, down payment, and military/service status.

Conventional
3–20% down. Best for 620+ scores with steady income. PMI drops off automatically at 78% loan-to-value. Most flexible property types.
FHA
3.5% down with 580+ credit (or 10% down from 500). Easier qualifying. Mortgage insurance for the life of the loan unless refinanced. Great for first-time buyers.
VA
0% down for active-duty, veterans, and qualifying spouses. No PMI. Competitive rates. One of the best programs available — if you qualify, use it.
USDA
0% down for eligible rural and many suburban areas. Income limits apply. Check your target neighborhoods on the USDA eligibility map.
Pro move: Get quotes from at least three lenders within a 14-day window — the credit bureaus treat them as a single inquiry. Compare APR (not just rate), fees, and lender credits.
Questions to ask every lender
✓What's the interest rate and the APR?
✓What are your origination, underwriting, and processing fees?
✓Will my rate be locked — and for how long?
✓Do you offer lender credits or down-payment-assistance pairing?
✓Who actually services the loan after I close?

A great buyer's agent isn't a door-opener — they're a strategist, negotiator, and project manager. In most markets they're paid by the transaction. The right agent saves you more than they cost, in price and headaches you'll never see.

What I actually do

Pull comps so you don't overpay (or underbid and lose).
Negotiate offer, repairs, credits, and concessions.
Coordinate inspections, title, lender, and closing.
Interview your agent

Build the search

Skills matter, but so does fit. You'll text this person at 9 p.m. when you're nervous about a counter-offer. Make sure that feels right.

Offer & Inspect

Tour smart. Offer cleanly. Inspect carefully.

Touring is fun — but it's also where buyers fall for the wrong thing. Walk every home with a checklist, write a clean offer, and never skip the inspection. Strong offers aren't always the highest — they're the cleanest.

Tour like a pro
Open windows — listen for noise.
Check water pressure & flush toilets.
Look for ceiling water stains, soft floors, odors.
Ask age of roof, furnace, water heater, panel.
What's in the offer
Price (based on comps, not list).
Earnest money 1–3%.
Closing date — match seller's timing if you can.
Contingencies: financing, appraisal, inspection.
Seller concessions toward closing costs.
Inspection options
Accept as-is.
Ask seller to repair or credit specific items.
Walk away (within your inspection window).
Pro Tip
Don't waive your inspection contingency. You can use 'inspect for information only' to stay competitive without giving up your right to walk.

Close & Move In

From clear-to-close to keys in hand.

Closing usually takes 60–90 minutes. There's a lot of paper, a few big signatures, and one very satisfying moment where someone hands you a key ring. Take the picture — you earned it.

Underwriting (weeks 1–3)
Lender re-verifies every document.
Appraisal is reviewed.
Homeowner's insurance is bound.
Title work completes → 'Clear to Close.'
Closing day
Final walkthrough 24 hours prior.
Bring photo ID + cashier's check / wire confirmation.
Sign loan docs (60–90 min).
Deed records. Seller is paid. You get the keys.
First 7 days as an owner
Set up electric, gas, water, internet.
Forward USPS mail; update license.
Re-key all exterior locks.
Test smoke & CO alarms; locate water shut-off.
Pro Tip
During the 21 days before closing: don't change jobs, take new loans, make big credit purchases, or co-sign anything. Lenders may re-pull credit at the end.
