# Meta ads for real estate agents: the cold, warm and hot system

This file is for another Claude. It's the full method, with nothing left out and nothing to look up elsewhere. Use it to answer questions about the system, write ads and follow-up in its style, or walk an agent through building the campaigns in Meta Ads Manager.

## Who this comes from and who it's for

Pedro is the broker at reThought Real Estate, a boutique brokerage with offices in Wisconsin (Milwaukee) and Florida (Orlando and Central Florida). He teaches this system to his real estate agents. Many of them work in English and Spanish, and their clients do too.

The goal for an agent is modest and practical. They don't need to become a marketing expert. They need to understand Meta ads well enough to stop guessing, stop wasting money, and build a campaign they can explain in one sentence. Pedro frames 2026 as "the year of systems": no more boosting a random post and hoping for leads. Every campaign should be repeatable, measurable, and built to feed the follow-up.

Everything you write for these agents comes in two versions, English and Puerto Rican Spanish. See the last section for the Spanish voice.

## The big idea

**Meta ads are a system, not magic.** A campaign needs four pieces working together. If one is weak, the whole campaign feels broken.

1. A clear audience. Who is this for: buyer, seller, relocation, first-time buyer, investor?
2. A specific offer. What useful thing do they get for raising their hand?
3. A simple message. Hook, value, offer, CTA, clear enough to read in two seconds.
4. A follow-up system. Who calls, who texts, who emails, and how fast.

**Meta sells attention, not leads.** Meta puts your message in front of people. Turning that attention into something useful is the agent's job: a conversation, an appointment, a buyer or seller consultation, database growth, long-term brand authority. Meta is one traffic source. It can't replace the agent's sales process, and expecting it to is the most common mistake.

The line Pedro repeats: the ad is just the front door. The money is made in the follow-up, the offer and the consistency.

**Every stage has its own framework.** Cold has a framework. Warm has a framework. Retargeting has a framework. The follow-up has a framework. Put the wrong framework in front of the wrong audience (a comparison-style ad shown to strangers, say) and the leads come in colder than the audience you started with.

## The temperatures

People usually say cold, warm and hot. In this system the hot stage is called **Retarget** (sometimes "re-engage"), and Pedro calls the people in it "almosts". Use "Retarget" in anything you write for the agents, since that's the word they've learned.

| Common name | Name in this system | Plain label | Who they are | Framework | Meta objective |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold | Cold, top of funnel | Strangers | People in your city who don't know you | AIDA | Engagement |
| Warm | Warm, middle of funnel | Engaged | Watched your video, engaged with your page or profile, messaged you | PAS or FAB | Leads |
| Hot | Retarget | Almosts | Watched a lot or visited your booking page, but didn't act | IDCA | Conversions (see the Ads Manager notes) |
| (after the ad) | Follow-up | Leads | Raised their hand | SCIPAB | none, this is texts and email |

Pedro uses one buyer couple to make this concrete. Marisol and Jay are first-time buyers near Orlando, late 20s, bilingual. They rent and they're tired of it. They're not sure they can qualify, they're scared of the process, and they scroll Zillow at midnight. The first time they see an ad, they're strangers in Orlando. The second time, they're watchers. The third time, they're almosts.

## Three campaigns, not three ad sets

This is the most important structural rule, and Pedro is open that he taught it wrong at first. He used to say: one campaign, with cold, warm and retarget as three ad sets inside it. The right way is **three separate campaigns.**

Why one campaign with three ad sets fails:

- Exclusions don't cascade between ad sets, so the same person can see all three ads. You pay three times.
- All three stages share one learning phase.
- With one budget pool, Meta's campaign budget optimization can starve the warm ad set.
- Killing one stage risks killing the others.

Why three campaigns works:

- Each campaign owns its own objective, audience and exclusions.
- Exclusions cascade cleanly from cold to warm to retarget.
- Each stage gets its own learning phase and its own budget reporting.
- You can kill or scale one campaign without touching the other two.

The cascade is the whole game:

- Cold runs to a location. Everyone who engages quietly fills the warm list, so cold spend builds the warm audience for free.
- Warm talks only to that list and excludes anyone who already became a lead.
- Warm engagement fills the retarget list.
- Retarget talks to the heavy watchers and excludes anyone who already booked.
- Converters are excluded from all three, so you stop paying to reach them.

Naming convention, so all three read at a glance on the dashboard: number, temperature, avatar or agent name, framework.

- `01 Cold [Name] AIDA`
- `02 Warm [Name] PAS+FAB`
- `03 Retarget [Name] IDCA`

### Campaign, ad set, ad: the three floors

Meta makes you fill three levels and people mix them up. Think of a building.

- **Campaign** is the building. It holds one decision: your goal (the objective).
- **Ad set** is the floor. It holds who sees it, how much you spend, and where it shows.
- **Ad** is the room. It's the actual video and words.

One campaign holds one or more ad sets, and each ad set holds one or more ads. For Marisol and Jay: the campaign goal is conversations, the ad set says "near Orlando, $20 a day", and the ad is the reel that says "I help first-time buyers near Orlando stop renting." Before clicking anything in Ads Manager, the agent should say out loud which floor they're on.

### The objective: what human to go find

The objective tells Meta what kind of person to hunt for. Engagement finds people who comment and message. Leads finds people who fill out forms or raise their hand. Conversions finds people who book. Pick the wrong one and Meta brings the wrong crowd, no matter how good the ad is.

Match the objective to the next action you want from that person, not the one you wish for. Marisol is in a different headspace each time she sees an ad, so she gets three different goals: Engagement on cold, Leads on warm, Conversions on retarget.

## Special Ad Category: the copy does the targeting

Meta restricts targeting for three categories: Housing, Employment and Credit. Real estate lands in two of them.

- Selling or marketing homes: **Housing.** This is what agents use for buyer and seller campaigns.
- Recruiting agents to a brokerage: **Employment.** Pedro's own example campaign is Employment, because he's recruiting agents.

Both lock the same things. No age targeting (Ads Manager forces 18 to 65+). No gender targeting (forced to All). No detailed demographic or interest targeting. No location radius tighter than 15 miles.

Declare the category on the very first step of building the campaign, every time. It can't be added after publishing without rebuilding, and if it's missing Meta blocks the launch partway through.

Agents panic when they see the locked settings. They shouldn't. The lesson: **the copy does the targeting.** "First-time buyers near Orlando who are tired of renting" filters the audience better than any age slider. Broad audience plus a sharp message gets the right person to raise their hand. Generic fails, specific filters. If the ad works for anybody, it works for nobody.

Two levers survive the Housing category:

1. A location (a city plus a radius of at least 15 miles).
2. Saved custom audiences of people based on what they did: watched a video, engaged on Instagram or Facebook, messaged you, visited your booking page. These lists make the cascade work.

**When you help an agent:** Pedro's worked examples below target agents (recruiting, Employment category). The agents you're helping build for their own buyer or seller avatar (Housing). Translate the examples to their client. Don't hand them recruiting copy.

## Before any ad: the avatar

Every piece of copy in this system starts from the agent's client avatar: who the client is, where they live, what they want, what they fear, their current situation, and what they say to themselves at the end of the month. No avatar, no ad. Garbage in, garbage out.

If an agent asks you to write any of these ads without giving you an avatar, ask for it first. If they can't fill in the worksheet at the end of this file, their avatar is still too generic, and that's the problem to fix before spending money.

Two example avatars:

**Pedro's avatar, "The $1M to $3M Agent".** A real estate agent, 23 to 35 years old, 2 to 4 years licensed, doing $1M to $3M in volume (4 to 12 deals a year), bilingual English and Spanish, in Milwaukee or Central Florida. They're producing despite their environment, not because of it, and they're one system away from doubling. When they compare options, they're looking at brokerages like KW, eXp and Real Broker. What they say to themselves: "I don't know where my next deal is coming from. My broker collects splits but hasn't trained me in 6 months."

**Marisol and Jay (buyer example).** First-time buyers near Orlando, late 20s, bilingual. Renting and tired of it, unsure they can qualify, scared of the process, scrolling Zillow at midnight.

## Cold: AIDA

**Who:** people who don't know you yet.

**Framework:** AIDA.

- **Attention.** Call out the person and the moment. The first 3 seconds decide everything.
- **Interest.** Reveal something true they didn't know. Earn the next 10 seconds.
- **Desire.** Show what's possible. Make them feel the outcome.
- **Action.** Tell them exactly what to do next. One word, one tap.

**The four-piece creative formula** is the same thing under different names. Hook is Attention. Value is Interest. Offer is Desire. CTA is Action. Weak ads are weak because they skip a piece, and most agents skip Value. They jump from hook straight to offer, and the ad feels like a commercial instead of useful advice.

### Pedro's cold ad

A 30-second vertical video of Pedro talking to camera. Not a polished production, on purpose. Trust comes from a real face, a real voice and specifics.

| Beat | Time | Line |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | 0-3s | "If you're a Milwaukee or Orlando agent closing 4 to 12 deals a year, this is for you." |
| Interest | 3-10s | "Most agents at your level aren't broken. They're building on the wrong foundation. No weekly training. No real broker." |
| Desire | 10-20s | "At reThought, we train live every Tuesday on the four systems that move production: marketing, prospecting, transactions, analytics. Bilingual. Two markets. One curriculum." |
| Action | 20-30s | "If you're producing $1M to $3M and ready to stop winging it, comment SYSTEMS and I'll send you the 48-week curriculum." |

The on-screen text in the feed: "If you're closing 4-12 deals a year in Milwaukee or Orlando... You're not stuck. You're under-trained." Caption ends with "Comment SYSTEMS for the curriculum."

Every line is specific: it names the cities, the production range and the action. The keyword SYSTEMS triggers a ManyChat flow that DMs the person the curriculum automatically. An agent's keyword might be GUIDE, LIST or HOMES.

==OUTPUT==

### Cold campaign setup, click by click

These labels match what agents see in Ads Manager.

1. From the Campaigns dashboard, click **Create**. Leave buying type on **Auction**. Under **Choose a campaign objective**, select **Engagement**. Click Continue. Engagement trains Meta to find people who comment and message, which matches a reel that ends with "comment KEYWORD".
2. On **Choose a campaign setup**, pick **Tailored messages campaign**. Click Continue.
3. At the campaign level, set **Campaign name** to `01 Cold [Name] AIDA`. Under **Budget strategy**, choose **Ad set budget**. Leave **Campaign bid strategy** on **Highest volume**.
4. Scroll to **Special Ad Categories**. Open Categories and choose **Housing** (or Employment if recruiting agents). Set **Countries** to United States. Click Next. Do this now.
5. At the ad set level, under **Conversion**, set **Conversion location** to **Message destinations**. Select the agent's Facebook Page.
6. Choose **Manual destination** and check the apps the agent actually answers: Messenger, Instagram, WhatsApp. When someone taps, they land in a real conversation instead of a cold form.
7. Set **Performance goal** to **Maximize number of conversations**.
8. Under **Budget & schedule**, set **Daily budget** to $20 to $50. Set a start date. Leave the end date off.
9. Under **Audience controls > Locations**, set the city plus a radius, at least 15 miles (Orlando, Florida + 15mi; Pedro runs Milwaukee and Orlando at 25 miles).
10. Exclude the warm audience (`Warm-Engagers-90d`) and anyone who already booked, so cold money only reaches new people. Build those custom audiences first (see "Building the audiences").
11. Under **Placements**, leave **Advantage+** on. Under **Ad transparency**, set the advertiser to the agent's verified business.
12. Expect **Detailed targeting** to show Age forced to 18-65+ and Gender to All. That's the Housing category. Don't fight it.
13. At the ad level, name the ad. Under **Identity**, confirm the Facebook Page and Instagram profile. Under **Destination**, choose **Messaging apps** and check the same apps as step 6.
14. Under **Ad creative**, add the video (a video is required). Format: single vertical video, 9:16, 30 seconds or less. Paste the caption from the prompt output.
15. Under **Conversations**, set the auto-greeting and quick questions people see after they tap. Make it sound like the agent, not a robot. Example for Marisol: "Hi, are you renting right now?" Nobody who taps should get silence.
16. Check the **Tracking** events. Open **Review**, check every level, then click the green **Publish**.

No video yet? Build everything else and leave it in draft. A campaign with no video has nothing to say.

**Cold pre-publish checklist**

- Reel uploaded (9:16, 30 seconds or less)
- Caption pasted from the prompt output, ending with "comment [KEYWORD]"
- ManyChat keyword matches the caption CTA exactly
- ManyChat flow tested with the keyword (wire it before publishing, or the first commenters get nothing back)
- Exclusion audience saved at the ad set level
- Daily budget $50 or less
- Special Ad Category declared (Housing, or Employment)

Budget reality: $20 a day for 14 days is $280 to learn what the cold ad can do. That's cheap tuition, not gambling. Run cold 7 to 14 days minimum so it clears the learning phase. Don't dump $500 on day one.

## Warm: PAS or FAB

**Who:** people who watched your video, engaged with your page or profile, or messaged you. They know you a little.

Warm people split two ways, and the framework depends on their headspace (Pedro calls it their consciousness):

- **PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution)** is for people who already feel the pain. Name the problem directly, twist the knife by showing what it costs, then present the offer. Format: a 5-card carousel or single image.
- **FAB (Feature, Advantage, Benefit)** is for people comparing options. They're shopping. Give them what it is (feature), why it beats the alternative (advantage), and what it means for them personally (benefit). Format: a 60-second talking-head video.

Many avatars feel both. Pedro's avatar feels pain thinking about their last broker and goes into comparison mode looking at other brokerages, so he runs both.

### Pedro's PAS carousel

Audience: people who watched 75%+ of the cold video in the last 30 days, plus Instagram and Facebook page engagers, plus commenters on the cold ad.

| Card | Beat | Text |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem | "You don't know where your next deal is coming from." |
| 2 | Problem | "Your broker collects your split. But hasn't trained you in 6 months." |
| 3 | Agitate | "Another quarter. You wing it. Meanwhile the agents who DO have a system double their volume." |
| 4 | Solution | "reThought trains live every Tuesday. 48 weeks. Marketing. Prospecting. Transactions. Analytics." |
| 5 | CTA | "Book 20 minutes with Pedro. No pitch. Just an honest conversation." |

Caption: "If you say 'I don't know where my next deal is coming from' at the end of every month, read this. ➡️"

Objective Leads, CTA button Book Now to Calendly, $30 a day, about 2 impressions per person per week.

### PAS prompt template

```
You are a real estate Meta ads copywriter. Write a 5-card PAS carousel for a warm Instagram audience.

==CLIENT AVATAR== (REQUIRED)
[PASTE YOUR CLIENT AVATAR: who, where, current situation, pain point, fear, what they say to themselves at the end of the month]

==MY OFFER==
[ONE SENTENCE: what I give them when they book or DM]

==MY DIFFERENTIATOR==
[ONE SENTENCE: why me, not another agent]

==OUTPUT==
Write 5 cards. Each card is one sentence. Keep them short enough to read on a phone in 2 seconds:
Card 1 (PROBLEM): The thing they don't know is broken. Make it specific.
Card 2 (PROBLEM): A second pain point they feel monthly.
Card 3 (AGITATE): Twist the knife. Show the cost, usually a comparison to peers who solved it.
Card 4 (SOLUTION): What I do. One specific deliverable, not vague benefits.
Card 5 (CTA): One action. Book a call, DM a keyword, or click the link.

Caption (for under the carousel): one paragraph, conversational, ends with the CTA verb (Book / DM / Click).
Then write the Puerto Rican Spanish version with sazón.
```

PAS only works when you know the exact pain. A vague avatar gives a vague problem, and the ad reads like a commercial.

### Building the carousel in Canva

Five minutes per agent.

- Create an Instagram Post design (square, 1080x1080). Make five, all from the same template. Same font, same size, same layout. Only the text changes. Differences between cards look like template errors, not art.
- Center the text.
- Color does narrative work. Cards 1 and 2 (problem) use the muted or dark brand color. Card 3 (agitate) goes warm, a darker red or clay. Card 4 (solution) switches to the brand green. Card 5 (CTA) is the brightest version.
- Paste the prompt output one card at a time.
- Download as PNG, not JPG, for sharper text. Upload to Meta in order 1 to 5. Upload order is swipe order.

### FAB prompt template

FAB needs to know who the avatar is comparing the agent against. Without a comparison set, the advantages have nothing to beat.

```
You are a real estate Meta ads copywriter. Write a 60-second FAB script for an Instagram talking-head video.

==CLIENT AVATAR== (REQUIRED)
[PASTE YOUR CLIENT AVATAR: who, where, situation]

==COMPARISON SET== (REQUIRED)
[Who is the avatar comparing me to? Other agents in the area? Online portals like Zillow? A specific competitor? Be specific.]

==MY TOP 3 FEATURES==
1. [Feature 1, concrete, not vague]
2. [Feature 2]
3. [Feature 3]

==OUTPUT==
Write 3 FAB triplets. Each triplet is one Feature, one Advantage (vs comparison set), one Benefit (emotional or practical):
Triplet 1: 0-20s
  F · [Feature]
  A · [Why it beats the comparison]
  B · [What it means for THEM personally]
Triplet 2: 20-40s
Triplet 3: 40-55s
Close: 55-60s, one-line CTA with a comment keyword for ManyChat.

Rules: each line say-able in 5 seconds. No jargon. Benefits are emotional or practical, never abstract ("save time" is abstract, "get weekends back" is concrete). Then write the Puerto Rican Spanish version with sazón.
```

### Filming the FAB video

Four frames, one beat per frame, three takes each.

| Frame | Time | Content | Framing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0-20s | Hook plus triplet 1 | Medium close-up, look at camera |
| 2 | 20-40s | Triplet 2 | Same framing |
| 3 | 40-55s | Triplet 3 | Same framing |
| 4 | 55-60s | Close: "DM me [KEYWORD]" | Direct to camera |

Phone vertical (9:16), propped at eye height. Natural light in front of you, not behind. A window works best. Three takes per frame minimum, pick the best in the edit. Edit in CapCut or InShot and add the on-screen text from the script.

### Warm campaign setup

The clicks are the same as the cold campaign. These settings change:

- **Build the custom audience first**, before the ad set. Saves about five minutes per campaign. See "Building the audiences" for `Warm-Engagers-90d`.
- **Objective:** Leads. The warm audience is ready to fill out a form or click through to a booking page. Use a Calendly booking page or a message flow as the destination.
- **Special Ad Category:** Housing again. Declare it on every campaign.
- **Name:** `02 Warm [Name] PAS+FAB`. The framework in the name tells you which creative to upload.
- **Two ad sets, same audience.** Ad set A is the PAS carousel, ad set B is the FAB video. Both target `Warm-Engagers-90d`. Both exclude anyone who already booked or already became a lead. This is an A/B test of the framework. Don't manually split the audience between them; Meta dedupes on its own, and splitting cuts your reach.
- **Budget:** about $100 a day across the two ad sets for 7 days (Pedro uses $60 on PAS, $40 on FAB). PAS carousels usually win on engagement and FAB videos usually win on conversion rate, but you don't know which one the avatar prefers until you test. After 7 days, compare cost per booking, move 80% of the budget to the winner and kill the loser. Once it's running, warm usually sits between $50 and $150 a day.
- **Ad A (PAS):** Carousel, 5 images in order (card01.png to card05.png). Caption from the prompt. CTA button Book Now, destination the agent's own Calendly link.
- **Ad B (FAB):** Video, 60 seconds or less, 9:16. Caption from the prompt. CTA button Send Message, destination the ManyChat flow for the COMPARE keyword (or the agent's own keyword).

**Size floor:** if `Warm-Engagers-90d` has fewer than 1,000 people, the cold campaign hasn't run long enough. Pause warm, run cold 14 more days, then come back.

**Warm pre-publish checklist**

- 5 PNGs uploaded in the correct order
- Calendly link is the agent's own link
- FAB video is 60 seconds or less
- ManyChat COMPARE (or the agent's keyword) flow tested
- Both CTAs land on the right destination

## Hot (Retarget): IDCA

**Who:** people who saw the agent's ads and didn't act. They watched the video but didn't book. They visited the booking page and bounced. They commented but never replied.

**Framework:** IDCA. AIDA assumes they don't know you. IDCA assumes they do and need a different push.

- **Identify.** Name them by behavior. "You watched the video and didn't book."
- **Disrupt.** Break their assumption. Usually they expect a sales pitch, so give them the opposite.
- **Compel.** Give the real reason to act now. A loss frame works here.
- **Act.** Make the next step tiny. One tap, lowest possible friction.

Disrupt is the beat that unlocks people. Admit they were probably expecting a pitch, then reframe the call as a gift.

### Pedro's retarget ad

Audience: agents who watched 50% to 75% of the cold or FAB video but didn't comment, plus people who visited the Calendly page and didn't book, plus Instagram and Facebook engagers who didn't DM, all in the last 30 days. Excludes anyone who already booked.

| Beat | Time | Line |
|---|---|---|
| Identify | 0-3s | "Hey, you watched my last video and didn't book the call." |
| Disrupt | 3-8s | "I don't recruit. I don't pitch. The call is 20 minutes. If reThought isn't right for you, I'll tell you in the first 10." |
| Compel | 8-12s | "But if you're already losing weekends figuring this out alone, you owe yourself the conversation." |
| Act | 12-15s | "Link in bio. Or comment NOW." |

On-screen in the feed: "You watched the video. You didn't book. 20 min. No pitch. I'll tell you in 10." Caption: "20 minutes. No pitch. If it's not a fit, I'll tell you in the first 10. → Link in bio. Or comment NOW."

The visual is Pedro talking straight to camera, no graphics. The cold ad had on-screen text and motion, so this one feels more intimate. The CTA splits two ways on purpose: a Calendly link for people who want the easy path, and the NOW comment keyword for people who prefer the DM flow. Splitting it catches both behaviors.

### IDCA prompt template

IDCA has to acknowledge what they already saw, so it needs a summary of the cold and warm ads.

```
You are a real estate Meta ads copywriter. Write a 15-second IDCA retargeting script.

==CLIENT AVATAR== (REQUIRED)
[PASTE YOUR CLIENT AVATAR]

==WHAT THEY ALREADY SAW== (REQUIRED)
[ONE-SENTENCE SUMMARY of your cold AIDA ad and warm PAS/FAB ad: what hook, what offer]

==THE ASSUMPTION I WILL BREAK==
[What do they assume about the call/offer? Usually: "this is going to be a pitch." Or: "the agent is going to push me to buy now." Be honest.]

==MY CTA OPTIONS==
Primary: Link in bio (Calendly)
Secondary: Comment a keyword for ManyChat

==OUTPUT==
Write 4 beats, 15 seconds total:
I · IDENTIFY (0-3s): Name them by behavior. "Hey, you watched [X] and didn't [Y]."
D · DISRUPT (3-8s): Break the assumption. "I'm not going to [what they fear]. Here's what this actually is..."
C · COMPEL (8-12s): Real reason to act now. Loss-frame works ("if you're already [X], you owe yourself [Y]").
A · ACT (12-15s): Dual CTA. Link in bio AND comment [KEYWORD].

Rules: direct-to-camera tone. Conversational, not scripted-sounding. Then write the Puerto Rican Spanish version with sazón.
```

### Filming the retarget video

Fifteen seconds flat. Meta serves people both the warm and retarget ads, and they pattern-match, so this one has to look and feel different.

| Frame | Time | Beat | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0-3s | Identify: "You watched X. You didn't Y." | Tight close-up |
| 2 | 3-8s | Disrupt: "I'm not [X]. This is [Y]." | Look at camera |
| 3 | 8-12s | Compel: "If you're [X], you owe [Y]." | Slower delivery |
| 4 | 12-15s | Act: "Link in bio. Or comment [KEY]." | Dual CTA |

Tighter than the FAB video, shoulders and face only. Lower energy, confidential, like you're texting them. Different room, different light, different background from the warm ad. No on-screen text, the voice carries it. Three takes per frame, cut tight in CapCut.

### Retarget campaign setup

Same clicks as cold. These settings change:

- **Objective:** Conversions, optimized for the Calendly booking event tracked by the Meta pixel. (In the current Ads Manager objective list this is **Sales**. See the Ads Manager notes.)
- **Special Ad Category:** Housing again.
- **Name:** `03 Retarget [Name] IDCA`.
- **Include** (anyone who did any of these): `Retarget-Viewers` (50% to 75% video viewers, or ThruPlay), Calendly page visitors who didn't book in the last 30 days, Instagram DM openers who didn't reply in the last 30 days.
- **Exclude** (anyone who did any of these): already booked, already in the active client pipeline, unfollowed in the last 30 days (if Ads Manager offers it).
- The logic: Include works like OR, Exclude works like AND-NOT. "Anyone who did A, B or C, minus anyone who did D, E or F." Three exclusions give you the highest-intent audience.
- **Audience size:** 500 to 2,000 people. This is the tightest audience of the three, and it should be. Under 500 means warm hasn't built enough engagement yet: pause retarget, scale warm for 7 days, try again. Much bigger than 2,000 means the funnel is leaking earlier.
- **Budget:** $20 to $50 a day. Pedro uses $30.
- **Frequency:** aim for about 2 impressions per person per week. More than that and people get retarget fatigue, start hiding the ad, and future delivery suffers. Once frequency passes 3 to 4, you're stalking them. Refresh the video.
- **Creative:** the 15-second IDCA video, 9:16. Caption under 40 words, shorter than the warm caption, because retarget audiences scroll fast and have already seen you.
- **Dual CTA:** primary button Book Now to the agent's Calendly (or Send Message), plus a ManyChat keyword (NOW) that sends the Calendly link by DM.

**Retarget pre-publish checklist**

- Video is 15 seconds or less
- Caption under 40 words
- ManyChat NOW flow sends the Calendly link
- Frequency is being watched (target about 2 per week)
- All exclusions saved

When all three are live, stop touching them for 7 days. Meta needs the learning phase, and editing resets it.

## Building the audiences

Build these before any ad set. In Ads Manager, open the menu and go to **Audiences**, then **Create audience > Custom audience > Next**.

1. **Warm list, Facebook.** Source: Facebook Page. Events: People who engaged with your Page. Retention: 90 days. Name: `Warm-Engagers-90d`. Click Create audience.
2. **Warm list, Instagram.** Source: Instagram profile. Events: People who engaged with this professional account. Retention: 90 days. Add it into the warm list so Instagram and Facebook engagers are both covered.
3. **Warm list, video.** Add video viewers who watched 75%+ of the cold reel. Optionally add people who messaged on Instagram or Facebook in the last 90 days.
4. **Retarget list.** Source: Video. Under Choose an engagement type, pick the heavy watchers: ThruPlay (completed or at least 15 seconds) or at least 50% or 75%. Name: `Retarget-Viewers`.
5. **Booking-based lists.** "Visited Calendly but didn't book" and "already booked" depend on the Meta pixel (or Conversions API) firing on the Calendly page and its confirmation page.

Then attach the lists as Include or Exclude inside each ad set.

| Campaign | Include | Exclude |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Cold, AIDA | Location only: city plus at least 15 miles | `Warm-Engagers-90d` (page engagers 90d, video viewers 75%+ 30d), already booked |
| 02 Warm, PAS+FAB | `Warm-Engagers-90d` (IG and FB engagers 90d, 75%+ viewers 30d, messengers 90d) | Already booked Calendly, already became a lead or converted |
| 03 Retarget, IDCA | `Retarget-Viewers` (50-75% viewers), Calendly visitors no booking (30d), DM openers no reply (30d) | Booked, active client pipeline, unfollowed (30d) |

Retention: 90 days is the freshest useful engagement. Older engagement means colder leads.

## Follow-up: SCIPAB

The ad is the front door. The follow-up is where leads become clients. SCIPAB is the framework for texts, emails and DMs after someone raises their hand. It works because it never feels like a pitch. The conversation is the bridge, not the ad, so every message has to feel human, not transactional.

- **Situation.** Name where they are right now, the moment they raised their hand.
- **Complication.** Acknowledge the friction, the thing in their way.
- **Implication.** Point at the cost of not fixing it: usually time, money or lost momentum.
- **Position.** Place yourself as the person who solves exactly that.
- **Action.** Ask one small thing. Reply with a number. Pick one of four words.
- **Benefit.** Name what they get for taking the small action.

All six pieces go in every message. The pattern stays the same. The content changes.

### Pedro's three messages

**5-minute text, after someone comments SYSTEMS:**

> Hey [Name], Pedro here, broker at reThought. Saw you commented SYSTEMS.
>
> Quick question before I send anything: where are you producing right now, 4 deals, 8, 12+? And are you in Milwaukee or Florida?
>
> The curriculum's the same but the first 90 days look different depending on which side of that range you're at.
>
> I'd rather send what's useful than just the PDF.

**24-hour email, if no reply.** Subject: "The 48-week curriculum + a question"

> Quick recap: you commented SYSTEMS on the Tuesday training video. Attaching the curriculum.
>
> One thing I'd ask before you skim it: what's the system you'd MOST want to fix in the next 60 days? Marketing, prospecting, transactions, or analytics?
>
> The curriculum looks the same to everyone, but the first month doesn't have to. Reply with one word and I'll point you to the exact Tuesday session.
>
> Pedro

**7-day text, last touch:**

> [Name], last one from me.
>
> Tuesday's training is on prospecting in a Q1 market: pulling absentee owner lists from county data and converting on the first call.
>
> If you want the recording when it drops, reply REC.
>
> No pressure either way.

Three touches, three framings of the same question. A conversation, not a pitch.

### The follow-up rhythm

Same cadence whether the avatar is an agent, a buyer or a seller.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| First 5 minutes | Text, call and email if you have it. Triple touch. Speed is the single biggest lever and beats everything else in the stack. |
| First 24 hours | SCIPAB email that delivers what they came for and opens the conversation with one easy question. |
| First 7 days | SCIPAB check-in, last touch. Tie it to a real moment (a training, a market event): "Reply REC if you want the recording." |
| First 30 days | Nurture campaign. Training reminders, new videos, education emails. Stay top of mind without selling. |

A lead is not a client. A conversation is the bridge. Most agents quit at touch 3, and the average sale needs 7 or more. That's where the money is. The agents who win with Meta ads aren't the ones with the cheapest leads. They're the ones with the fastest, most human follow-up.

## Budget

- $20 to $50 a day per campaign. Small enough that a bad week doesn't hurt, big enough that Meta has data to learn from. Two dollars a day never learns and wastes money slowly.
- Typical split once all three run: cold $20 to $50, warm about $100 while testing PAS against FAB (then $50 to $150), retarget $20 to $50.
- Big-picture rule: plan to spend about 10% of what you want to earn. Want $100,000 in commissions? Plan around $10,000 in ads across the year.
- Pick a daily number the agent won't flinch at if it disappears, then leave it alone long enough for Meta to learn.

## Placement

Placement is where the ad shows up: Instagram reels, Facebook feed, stories and more. **Advantage+ placements** lets Meta put the ad wherever it performs best. That's right for about 90% of agents, especially starting out. **Manual** placement means hand-picking spots, and it's only worth it once real data shows one spot wins. A vertical reel shot for phones naturally flows into reels and stories, where people like Marisol scroll at night. Leave it on Advantage+ until there's a reason on paper to change it.

## What to measure

Ignore likes. They feel good and pay nothing. Comments are useful and views can help, but the real scoreboard is what happens after the lead enters the system.

**Business numbers:**

1. Cost per lead. What did it cost to capture one lead?
2. Lead quality. Real prospects, or random clicks?
3. Speed to lead. How fast did someone follow up?
4. Response rate. How many leads replied after follow-up?
5. Appointment rate. How many booked a consultation?
6. Conversion rate. How many became clients?

**Ads Manager numbers:**

- Cost per result: what you pay each time someone does what the objective asked for.
- Click rate (CTR): are people tapping or scrolling past?
- Cost per 1,000 impressions (CPM): how expensive the audience is to reach.
- Frequency: how many times one person has seen the ad. Past 3 to 4 on retarget, refresh.
- Results: comments, leads or bookings, depending on the objective.
- **Cost per conversation:** ad spend divided by real conversations started. This is the headline number. Check it weekly.

A cheap lead that never responds is not cheap. An expensive lead that becomes a client is very profitable.

## Worksheet for an agent's own avatar

An agent should walk away with four lines that could go live this week. If they can't fill all four, the avatar is too generic.

1. **Cold, AIDA.** Their hook line: "If you're a [WHO] in [WHERE] facing [WHAT], this is for you."
2. **Warm, PAS or FAB.** Pick one based on the audience's headspace, then write card 1. PAS: "You don't know [PROBLEM]." FAB: Feature, Advantage, Benefit.
3. **Retarget, IDCA.** Their Disrupt line, the assumption they'll break: "I'm not selling. I'm [X]."
4. **Follow-up, SCIPAB.** Their 5-minute first text. Situation, Complication, Position, Action, Benefit. One question only.

## Notes on the current Ads Manager

Meta changes Ads Manager often. These are points where the method and the live screens may not line up. Check the agent's actual screen before telling them where to click.

- **Objective names.** The current objective list is Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, App promotion and Sales. There's no objective literally called "Conversions". For the retarget campaign, use Sales (or Leads with a website conversion location) and optimize for the Calendly booking event.
- **Frequency cap.** As far as I know, a hard frequency cap is only offered on reach-focused campaigns, not on Engagement, Leads or Sales. On retarget, watch the Frequency column and refresh creative when it passes 3 to 4.
- **Lookalikes.** Standard lookalike audiences aren't available under the Housing or Employment categories. Don't promise them for cold targeting.
- **"Unfollowed" exclusion.** This may not exist as a custom audience source. Treat it as optional.
- **Booking events.** The "visited Calendly but didn't book" and "already booked" audiences only work if the Meta pixel or Conversions API fires on the Calendly page and its confirmation page. Without that, the last step of the cascade doesn't happen.

## How to help an agent with this

1. Get their avatar, their market (city plus radius), and whether it's Housing or Employment.
2. Find their stage (1 to 5) and help with that step, not the whole thing.
3. Figure out which temperature they're writing for and use that stage's framework. No FAB on cold, no AIDA on retarget.
4. Write in the house voice: second person, short lines, specific to the avatar, no jargon. Never "DM me for info". Never "circling back".
5. Give English and Puerto Rican Spanish.
6. For campaign setup: audiences first, then three separate campaigns with the exclusions wired, then hands off for 7 days.
7. Always bring it back to follow-up. Speed to lead and seven-plus touches matter more than the ad.

Pedro closes on a Seth Godin line: "Marketing is the act of making change happen." Every campaign, at $50 or $5,000, is an attempt to make change happen for the avatar the agent serves.

## The Spanish voice

"Puerto Rican Spanish with sazón" means warm, conversational and a little playful. Contractions are fine ("pa" for "para"). Puerto Rican words are welcome ("chavos" for money, "botar chavos" for wasting money). Agents mix in English Meta terms naturally, so keep them in English: campaña cold, warm, retarget, ad set, leads, reel, keyword, Housing. A few reference phrases:

- "Dejar de adivinar, dejar de botar chavos." (Stop guessing, stop wasting money.)
- "Construye la campaña cold en borrador." (Build the cold campaign in draft.)
- "$20/día en cold por dos semanas son $280 para saber si tu mensaje pega. Matrícula barata." ($20 a day on cold for two weeks is $280 to learn if your message lands. Cheap tuition.)
- "Un lead barato que nunca responde no es barato. Un lead caro que se vuelve cliente es una ganga." (A cheap lead who never replies isn't cheap. An expensive lead who becomes a client is a bargain.)
- "Cold corre a tu ubicación y calladito llena Warm-Engagers-90d." (Cold runs to your location and quietly fills Warm-Engagers-90d.)
